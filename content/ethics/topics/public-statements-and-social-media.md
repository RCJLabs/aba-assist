ourSummary: >-
  Anything you communicate to the public about your professional activities is covered:
  advertising, a website, a conference talk, a comment to a reporter, and — the one that
  catches people — social media. Two obligations run through all of it. First, be
  truthful: do not overstate your credentials, do not describe yourself in a way that
  implies a certification you do not hold, and do not claim outcomes the evidence does
  not support. Second, protect the people you serve: client information is not content,
  images and stories need consent, and the analyst code additionally restricts soliciting
  testimonials from current clients, because someone currently depending on you for
  services is not in a position to say no.
plainSummary: >-
  Be honest about what you are and what your work achieves. Do not post about clients.
  A story with no name can still identify a child in a small town.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Your profile says exactly which certification you hold, and does not imply a higher one.
  - No session photos, no "today a kid I work with..." posts, however affectionate.
  - Marketing claims that match what the evidence supports, not what sells.
  - Testimonials, if used at all, from former clients who gave informed consent.
commonPitfalls:
  - Believing a post is anonymous because no name appears; setting, timing and detail identify people.
  - Asking current families for reviews, sometimes with an incentive attached.
  - Describing the profession or a procedure in a way that implies guaranteed outcomes.
  - Letting an employer's marketing describe your credentials inaccurately without correcting it.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Assume the family will read it, because eventually someone will. If a post needs a
  detail removed to be safe, it usually needs not to exist. Check your employer's media
  policy, and ask your supervisor before anything public involving a client.

Certification marks belong to the board that issues them, and misusing them is its own
problem on top of the ethics one. "RBT" after your name means you currently hold that
certification — not that you once did, and not that you are working toward it.
