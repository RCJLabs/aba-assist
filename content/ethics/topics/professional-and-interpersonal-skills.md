ourSummary: >-
  The technician outline treats interpersonal skill as an ethics task, not a soft extra,
  and it names the components: accepting feedback, listening actively, seeking input, and
  collaborating. The reason is that these behaviours are what make the rest of the system
  work. A technician who takes feedback well gets better supervision. One who asks
  questions surfaces problems while they are small. One who collaborates with a teacher or
  a speech therapist stops a learner being taught two contradictory things. The analyst
  code puts the same obligation on analysts and adds coordinating with other providers in
  a client's life.
plainSummary: >-
  Take feedback without defending. Listen to families and colleagues. Ask when you are
  unsure. Work with the other people in the learner's life.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Hearing a correction and asking a clarifying question instead of explaining why you did it.
  - Writing down what a caregiver raised and passing it on the same day.
  - Agreeing a single communication system with a speech-language pathologist rather than running two.
  - Saying you do not know, and finding out.
commonPitfalls:
  - Treating feedback as criticism, which trains your supervisor to stop giving it.
  - Answering a caregiver's clinical question yourself to seem competent.
  - Going quiet about a problem because raising it feels like admitting fault.
  - Dismissing another discipline's input as unscientific rather than coordinating with it.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Default to passing information along rather than holding it. Almost nothing is made
  worse by your supervisor knowing about it sooner.
