ourSummary: >-
  Informed consent means the client or their legal representative agreed to assessment or
  treatment after being told, in language they understand, what will happen, why, what the
  risks and benefits are, what the alternatives are, and that they may withdraw. It is
  obtained before services start and again when the plan changes materially — a new
  restrictive procedure, a functional analysis, a video recording. It is distinct from
  assent, which is the person's own moment-to-moment willingness. It also raises the
  question of who the client actually is: the person receiving services, the parent, the
  school district paying, and the funder can all have different interests, and the code
  requires you to identify them and act in the client's.
plainSummary: >-
  Get permission before you start, from the person who can legally give it, after
  explaining it plainly. Ask again when the plan changes a lot. Be clear who the client is.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Explaining a functional analysis and its risks to a parent before running one.
  - A service agreement that says what will be provided, by whom, and how it ends.
  - Noticing when a school's goal and a family's goal differ, and naming it.
  - Re-consenting before recording video, even for training.
commonPitfalls:
  - Treating the intake signature as consent for everything that follows.
  - Explaining in professional language and counting understanding as given.
  - Letting the payer's priorities quietly become the treatment goals.
  - Confusing assent with consent, or using one to excuse the absence of the other.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Ask whether this person would be surprised by what is about to happen. If they might be,
  it needs explaining and agreeing first.
