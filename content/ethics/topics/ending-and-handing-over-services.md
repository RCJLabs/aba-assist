ourSummary: >-
  Services end for many reasons — goals met, a family moves, funding stops, a practitioner
  leaves — and the obligation is the same in each: plan it, give notice, document where
  things stand, and hand over to someone qualified. Leaving a position without arranging
  continuity is serious enough that the handbooks list it among the events a certificant
  must self-report. For a technician the version is smaller but real: give the notice your
  employer requires, and walk the incoming technician and the supervisor through the
  current programs rather than leaving a file. Discharge when services are no longer
  needed is also part of this: continuing because it is convenient or billable is its own
  failure.
plainSummary: >-
  When you leave or services end, give notice, write down where things stand, and hand
  over properly. Nobody should be left without a plan or a provider.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Notice given in time for a real handover, not on the last day.
  - A written summary of current programs, data and next steps for whoever takes over.
  - A transition meeting including the family, not just the professionals.
  - Discharging when goals are met rather than continuing out of habit.
commonPitfalls:
  - Delaying the announcement to avoid unsettling families, which removes the time to plan.
  - Treating a file as a handover.
  - Taking records with you when you go.
  - Continuing services that are no longer needed because the funding is authorised.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Ask your supervisor what your organisation's notice and transition process requires, and
  start it earlier than feels necessary. The families need the lead time more than you do.
