ourSummary: >-
  Certificants must tell the board about certain events concerning themselves, generally
  within 30 days of becoming aware of them. The categories are broadly: violations of the
  ethics code; supervision non-compliance; investigations naming you or a company you own
  or run; certain legal and disciplinary outcomes; and health conditions or substance use
  disorders that genuinely affect your ability to practise safely. Changes to your name,
  address and email are also required within 30 days. Two things people get wrong: this is
  separate from reporting someone else's alleged violation, which is a different process;
  and the board's published guidance says that when you are unsure whether something
  qualifies, the better course is to report it.
plainSummary: >-
  Tell the board within 30 days about things like an investigation, a supervision lapse,
  or a health condition that affects safe practice. When in doubt, report it.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Reporting a month without required supervision as soon as you notice it.
  - Updating your portal account within 30 days of moving or changing your name.
  - Self-reporting an employer investigation without waiting for its outcome.
  - Checking the board's self-reporting guidance rather than guessing.
commonPitfalls:
  - Waiting for an investigation to conclude before reporting it.
  - Assuming your employer reported it on your behalf.
  - Treating a supervision gap as an administrative matter rather than a reportable one.
  - Reading the categories narrowly to avoid a report you dread making.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Read the board's self-reporting page and, if it is still unclear, report. A report that
  turns out to be unnecessary is dismissed; an unreported event that surfaces later is a
  separate violation on top of the original one.
