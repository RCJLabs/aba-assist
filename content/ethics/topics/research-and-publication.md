ourSummary: >-
  The analyst code devotes a whole section to research, and it applies more often than
  people expect: evaluating a procedure with your own clients and writing it up is
  research, not just practice. The obligations are to follow applicable law and review
  requirements, obtain informed consent from participants separately from consent for
  services, protect confidentiality in the data and in what gets published, work within
  your competence, manage conflicts of interest, credit contributions honestly and in
  proportion to the work done, avoid plagiarism, and retain and report data accurately.
  Where research happens inside service delivery, the client's interests come first: a
  participant who withdraws must still receive appropriate services.
plainSummary: >-
  Get proper approval and separate consent before doing research. Protect participants'
  privacy. Credit people fairly. Report the data as they are.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Seeking review before evaluating a procedure with your caseload for publication.
  - Consent for research obtained separately from consent for treatment.
  - Authorship discussed early and based on contribution.
  - Data retained, and reported including the results that did not work out.
commonPitfalls:
  - Assuming that because the work is with your own clients, it is only practice.
  - Adding a senior colleague as an author by default.
  - Reporting only the participants whose data were clean.
  - Reusing your own earlier text without citation.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Ask whether you intend to generalize or disseminate the findings. If yes, treat it as
  research and get review before you start rather than at submission.
