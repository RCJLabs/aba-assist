ourSummary: >-
  What you write is used to make clinical decisions, to justify payment, and sometimes in
  legal proceedings. All three uses require the same thing: that it describes what
  actually happened, in observable terms, recorded close to the event. Estimating data
  from memory, filling a gap with last session's numbers, recording scheduled time rather
  than delivered time, or writing a note before the session are all falsification, whether
  or not anyone intended fraud. Gaps and errors are normal; the honest handling of them is
  to mark the gap, say what happened, and tell your supervisor. That is a small
  conversation. The alternative is an integrity finding, which is not.
plainSummary: >-
  Write what actually happened, as you saw it, soon after. Record the time you really
  delivered. If data are missing, say so rather than filling them in.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Notes completed the same day, in observable language, with times and counts.
  - A visible, explained gap where recording failed.
  - Session time recorded as delivered, even when the family ended early.
  - Telling your supervisor about an error before anyone else finds it.
commonPitfalls:
  - Reconstructing numbers from memory, which looks identical to real data on a graph.
  - Writing interpretations or motives instead of observations.
  - Billing the scheduled duration rather than the delivered one.
  - Leaving a gap blank and unexplained, so it reads as zero occurrences.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Write only what you can stand behind, note what is uncertain, and ask your supervisor
  how they want the gap recorded. Never resolve uncertainty by guessing a number.

supervisor reading invented data will make a real decision about a real person on the
basis of something that did not happen.
