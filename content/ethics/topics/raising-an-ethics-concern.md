ourSummary: >-
  When you see something that concerns you, the usual expectation is to address it
  directly with the person where doing so is appropriate and safe, then through your
  organization, and to the certifying board where it is serious or unresolved. The board
  publishes guidance on when a formal notice is the right step, and it is deliberately not
  the first step for everything. Two situations override the gradual approach: anything
  involving danger to a person, and anything you are separately required by law to report,
  such as suspected abuse. Power matters here — a technician raising a concern about their
  own supervisor is not in the same position as a peer raising it, and organizations
  should have a route that does not run through the person concerned.
plainSummary: >-
  Where it is safe, raise it with the person first, then your organization. Go to the
  board for serious or unresolved problems. Danger and legal reports go straight there.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Asking a colleague directly about something you saw, before assuming the worst.
  - Using your organization's route when a direct conversation is not appropriate.
  - Documenting what you observed factually and when.
  - Going straight to the authorities where someone is at risk.
commonPitfalls:
  - Staying silent because the person is senior to you.
  - Discussing it with other colleagues rather than through a proper route.
  - Filing formally over something a two-minute conversation would have resolved.
  - Treating "handle it internally" as a reason not to make a legally required report.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Ask your supervisor how to raise it, or if the concern is about your supervisor, ask
  whoever your organization designates instead. Write down what you observed while it is
  fresh, in objective terms.
