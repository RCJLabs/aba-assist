ourSummary: >-
  Both codes draw a line between what your certification permits and what you personally
  can do well. The second is always narrower, and it is the one that governs. For a
  technician this means delivering a procedure only after being trained on it and shown
  to perform it correctly — which in practice means instructions, a demonstration,
  practice, and feedback until you meet a standard. For an analyst it means the same
  applied to populations, settings and procedures: experience with young children in
  homes does not make you competent with adults who have feeding disorders. Building
  competence in a new area is allowed and expected; doing it on a client without training
  or supervision is not.
plainSummary: >-
  Do only what you have been trained and checked on. Your certificate says what you may
  do. Your training says what you can do well. Ask before you try something new.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - A new procedure appears in a plan, and you ask to be trained on it before the next session.
  - You say plainly that you have never run a preference assessment of that type.
  - An analyst gets supervision from a specialist before accepting a case outside their experience.
  - You practise a procedure in a role play before doing it with a learner.
commonPitfalls:
  - Reading about a procedure and counting that as training.
  - Running something unfamiliar because the supervisor wrote it, as if authorship transferred competence.
  - Quietly skipping the part of a plan you are unsure about, which is both an unauthorised change and a documentation problem.
  - Assuming competence with one learner transfers to a learner who is nothing like them.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Tell your supervisor which specific step you have not been trained on, and ask for
  a demonstration and a practice round. Supervisors expect this; it is a much smaller
  conversation than an incident report.

The phrase worth keeping is that a credential is permission, not proof. The board
certifies that you met a set of requirements on a particular date. Whether you can run
this procedure with this learner tomorrow is a different question, and only your
supervisor and your own honest answer settle it.
