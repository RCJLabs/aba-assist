ourSummary: >-
  Two connected obligations. Cultural humility is the stance: keep examining your own
  assumptions, notice where your background differs from the family's, and learn about
  their values from them rather than from a summary about a group. Cultural
  responsiveness is what follows from it: adapting goals, procedures, materials and
  language so services fit this family's actual life. The technician outline asks for both
  — ongoing humility and responsiveness including identifying personal biases — in service
  delivery and in professional relationships. The analyst code extends it to supervision,
  where identity and power shape who gets coached and who gets overlooked.
plainSummary: >-
  Notice your own assumptions. Ask each family what matters to them. Then change how you
  work so it fits their life, their language and their routines.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Asking a family how mealtimes, greetings or bedtime actually work in their home before setting goals.
  - Choosing reinforcers from what the family eats and plays with.
  - Noticing that you coach one supervisee more than another and asking yourself why.
  - Materials and instructions available in the language spoken at home.
commonPitfalls:
  - Reading about a culture and applying it as a template to every family from it.
  - Treating "the same for everyone" as fairness when it ignores real differences.
  - Setting goals that serve the setting's convenience rather than the family's values.
  - Avoiding the subject entirely so as not to say the wrong thing, which leaves assumptions unexamined.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-
  Ask the family directly and treat their answer as the authority on their own life. Where
  a goal seems to conflict with family values, raise it with your supervisor rather than
  quietly deciding either way.
