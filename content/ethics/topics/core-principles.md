ourSummary: >-
  Both codes are built on the same handful of principles: benefit the people you serve
  and avoid harming them; treat them with compassion, dignity and respect; act with
  integrity; and work within your competence. Every specific standard is one of those
  four worked out for a particular situation, which is why the principles are the right
  thing to reach for when a situation is not covered by anything specific. They are also
  how the exam asks about ethics: the technician outline's first ethics task is
  recognizing these principles and applying them, not reciting standard numbers.
plainSummary: >-
  Do good and avoid harm. Treat people with respect. Be honest. Work inside your training.
  Every other rule comes from one of these four ideas.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - You hit a situation no rule covers, and you ask which choice actually benefits the learner.
  - You catch yourself about to talk about a learner in front of them, and you stop.
  - You tell your supervisor about your own mistake before anyone else finds it.
  - You say "I have not been trained on that" instead of improvising.
commonPitfalls:
  - Treating ethics as a list to memorize for an exam rather than a way to decide in the moment.
  - Assuming that because something is not explicitly forbidden, it is fine.
  - Reading "benefit others" as "keep everyone happy"; the client's interests come first, not the organization's.

  competence is a question a supervisor can answer in a minute.
