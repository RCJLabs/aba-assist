ourSummary: >-
  Some situations are outside what any reference can answer: someone is in danger, a
  person is injured, a learner leaves toward traffic or water, a medical emergency, a
  disclosure or suspicion of abuse, a statement about wanting to die. In each, the right
  action is to get help from the people and systems that exist for it — emergency
  services, your site's crisis procedure, the learner's own plan, your supervisor — and
  then to document what happened. The technician outline includes implementing crisis and
  emergency procedures as a task precisely because those procedures are local: they come
  from your employer and the learner's plan, and they are what you follow. Knowing them
  before you need them is the actual preparation.
plainSummary: >-
  If someone is in danger, get help first. Follow your workplace's crisis plan and the
  learner's plan. Then write down what happened and tell your supervisor the same day.
whatThisLooksLike:
  - Knowing your site's crisis procedure before your first session with a learner who has a history.
  - Calling emergency services rather than working out whether it is serious enough.
  - Completing the incident report before you leave for the day.
  - Reporting any injury, to a client or to yourself, the same day however minor.
commonPitfalls:
  - Trying to manage a crisis alone to avoid making a fuss.
  - Treating a behaviour plan as if it overrode a medical one.
  - Waiting for certainty before making a mandated report.
  - Leaving the incident report until tomorrow, when the detail has gone.
ifYouAreUnsure: >-

  delayed one is not. The urgent page in this app lists who to contact and what to record
  for each of these situations.
