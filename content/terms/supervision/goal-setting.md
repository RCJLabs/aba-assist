id: goal-setting
term: Goal Setting
aliases: [self-set goals, performance goals]
category: supervision
definition:
  technical: >-
    Specifying a level of performance to be reached, stated so that reaching it can be
    judged from the data. As a performance-management procedure it is an antecedent
    intervention, and it is routinely combined with feedback, because a goal with no
    information about progress toward it has little to act on.
  plain: >-
    Agree a specific number to aim for, not "do better". Then show progress against it.
  gloss: Naming a specific level of performance to reach
examples:
  - text: >-
      A supervisor and supervisee agree the supervisee will run 90% of trials to protocol
      by the end of the month, measured from integrity checks already being taken.
    setting: clinic
    why: Specific, measurable from data that exist, and tied to a date.
  - text: >-
      Players set their own targets for correct play execution, and results are posted
      against them. Self-set goals with posted feedback outperformed either alone.
    setting: community
    why: The combination is what the sport-performance study examined.
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      "Be more professional this quarter." Nothing here says what would count as having
      done it, so no data can show whether it happened.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      A supervisor announcing a target nobody discussed, measured by something nobody
      records. A goal that cannot be tracked is a statement of hope.
    setting: clinic

The supervision task list asks for goals that are measurable, individualised and matched
to what the supervisee still needs to learn — which rules out both the goal everyone gets
and the goal copied from last year.

Who sets the goal is a real variable, not a courtesy. Self-set goals have been shown to
work, and involving the person in setting them costs nothing; a goal handed down is easier
to write and easier to ignore.
