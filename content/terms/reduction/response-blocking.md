id: response-blocking
term: Response Blocking
aliases: [response interruption]
category: reduction
definition:
  technical: >-
    A procedure in which the interventionist physically interrupts a response as it
    begins so that it cannot be completed, used for behavior that is dangerous or
    automatically reinforced. It involves physical contact with the person, so it
    appears in a plan only when the supervising analyst has written it in, staff have
    been trained in it hands-on, and it complies with employer policy and law. This
    entry describes what the term means and does not describe how to do it.
  plain: >-
    Stopping a behavior from being completed by physically getting in its way. Because
    it involves touching the person, it is only ever done as written in a plan by
    trained staff. This entry does not teach it.
  gloss: Physically interrupting a response; plan-and-training only
examples:
  - text: >-
      A written plan, with hands-on training, directs staff to interrupt a learner's
      hand moving toward their own eye.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Moving a breakable object out of a learner's reach before a session. Changing the
      environment in advance is an antecedent strategy, not response blocking.
    setting: clinic
