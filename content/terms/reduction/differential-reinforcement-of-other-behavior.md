id: differential-reinforcement-of-other-behavior
term: Differential Reinforcement of Other Behavior
aliases: [DRO, omission training]
abbreviation: DRO
category: reduction
definition:
  technical: >-
    A procedure in which reinforcement is delivered at the end of an interval during which
    a specified behavior did not occur. It reinforces the absence of that behavior rather
    than any particular replacement, so it teaches nothing new on its own.
  plain: >-
    A reward is given when a set amount of time passes without the behavior. It rewards
    the behavior not happening. It does not teach a new skill by itself.
  gloss: Reinforcing time passing without the behavior
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner earns a preferred activity after each five minutes in which shouting did
      not occur.
    setting: school
  - text: >-
      Under a resetting DRO the timer starts again the moment the behavior occurs, so the
      learner has to go a full interval without it to earn anything.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Reinforcing a learner for handing over a break card, which reinforces a specific
      alternative rather than the absence of behavior.
    setting: clinic
    why: That is differential reinforcement of an alternative behavior.
  - text: >-
      Giving a reward on a fixed schedule regardless of what happened, which is not
      differential at all.
    setting: home

Because DRO reinforces an absence, it is usually paired with teaching something to do
instead. A plan that reduces a behavior without building a replacement leaves the learner
without a way to get what the behavior was getting.
