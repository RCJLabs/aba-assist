term: Antecedent Intervention
aliases: [antecedent strategy, antecedent manipulation, proactive strategy]
category: reduction
definition:
  technical: >-
    A change made to the environment or the conditions preceding a behavior so that the
    behavior becomes less likely to occur at all, rather than being addressed after it
    happens. Examples include altering task demands, offering choice, and adjusting
    motivating conditions.
  plain: >-
    Changing things before the behavior happens so it is less likely in the first place.
    You adjust the setting or the task instead of waiting and reacting.
  gloss: Changing conditions so the behavior is less likely
examples:
  - text: >-
      A written plan schedules a break every ten minutes rather than waiting for a learner
      to become distressed.
    setting: school
  - text: Offering a choice between two tasks, both of which meet the same goal.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Withholding the consequence that has maintained a behavior, which acts after the
      behavior rather than before it.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      Adding a reward after a behavior occurs. That is a consequence, whatever else it
      does.

Antecedent strategies are often the least intrusive part of a plan and the easiest to run
consistently, which is why they tend to appear first. Which ones apply to a learner is
still a decision made from their assessment.
