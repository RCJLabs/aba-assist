term: Differential Reinforcement of Alternative Behavior
aliases: [DRA, functional communication training, FCT]
abbreviation: DRA
category: reduction
definition:
  technical: >-
    A procedure in which reinforcement is delivered for a specified alternative response
    that serves the same function as a target behavior, while reinforcement for the target
    behavior is withheld or reduced. Functional communication training is the widely used
    variant in which the alternative response is a communicative one.
  plain: >-
    You teach a different way to get the same result, and make sure that way works well.
    The old behavior stops paying off as well as the new one does.
  gloss: Reinforce a better way to get the same result
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner who has been leaving the table to escape work is taught to hand over a
      break card, and the break is given promptly every time the card is used.
    setting: clinic
    why: The alternative produces the same outcome the original behavior produced.
  - text: >-
      A learner learns to tap a staff member's shoulder for attention instead of shouting,
      and tapping is answered immediately.
    setting: school
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Teaching a learner to request a snack when the behavior of concern was maintained by
      escape from work. The alternative does not match the function.
    setting: clinic
    why: A functionally irrelevant alternative will not compete with the target behavior.
  - text: >-
      Reinforcing a new response while the old behavior still works just as well and just
      as quickly. Without a difference in payoff there is nothing differential about it.
    setting: home

The alternative has to be genuinely easier and more reliable than the behavior it
replaces. If handing over a card takes longer to produce a break than leaving the table
does, the card will lose.

Selecting the alternative response is the supervising analyst's decision, because it
depends on the function identified during assessment.
