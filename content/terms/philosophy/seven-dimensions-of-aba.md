term: Seven Dimensions of ABA
aliases: [dimensions of applied behavior analysis, Baer Wolf and Risley dimensions]
category: philosophy
definition:
  technical: >-
    The criteria proposed in 1968 for what makes a piece of work applied behavior
    analysis: it addresses socially important behavior (applied), measures real behavior
    (behavioral), demonstrates that the intervention caused the change (analytic),
    describes its procedures precisely (technological), ties them to basic principles
    (conceptually systematic), produces change large enough to matter (effective), and
    produces change that lasts and spreads (generality).
  plain: >-
    Seven tests a program should pass to count as ABA. It must matter to the person,
    measure real behavior, prove it worked, be described clearly, link to principles,
    make a big enough difference, and last.
  gloss: The seven criteria that define applied behavior analysis
examples:
  - text: >-
      A written program that names the target, the procedure, the principle it relies on,
      and the data that will show whether it worked meets most of the dimensions on paper.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A program described only as "positive strategies" with no procedure anyone could
      repeat. It fails the technological dimension whatever else it does.
    setting: school
