term: Applied Behavior Analysis
aliases: [ABA]
abbreviation: ABA
category: philosophy
definition:
  technical: >-
    The branch of behavior analysis that applies the principles of learning to behavior
    that matters in a person's life, and uses experimental methods to show that the
    change came from the intervention rather than from something else. It is defined by
    a set of dimensions rather than by any particular technique or population.
  plain: >-
    Using what we know about learning to help with behavior that matters to a person.
    It also checks, with data, that the help actually worked.
  gloss: The applied branch of the science of behavior
examples:
  - text: >-
      A school team teaches a student to request a break, measures the requests and the
      problem behavior they replace, and shows the change held once teaching stopped.
    setting: school
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A study of how rats respond under a new reinforcement schedule. That is the
      experimental analysis of behavior, which asks basic rather than applied questions.
    setting: any
  - text: >-
      A rewards chart used in a classroom with no measurement and no way to tell whether
      it changed anything. Using reinforcement is not the same as doing behavior analysis.
    setting: school
