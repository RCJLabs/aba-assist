term: Negative Reinforcement
aliases: [escape contingency, avoidance contingency, SR-]
abbreviation: SR-
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    A contingency in which a stimulus is removed, reduced, or postponed immediately
    following a response, and the future frequency of that response increases as a
    result. The defining feature is the increase in responding, not the pleasantness of
    what was removed. Escape describes removal of a stimulus already present; avoidance
    describes postponing or preventing one.
  plain: >-
    Something goes away right after a behavior, and the person does that behavior more
    often from then on. Negative here means something was taken away. It does not mean
    bad.
  gloss: Taking something away makes a behavior happen more
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner signs "break," the worksheet is put away, and over the next week they
      sign "break" more often when work is presented.
    setting: school
    why: The worksheet was removed and signing increased, so the contingency reinforced signing.
  - text: >-
      You put on a coat before going outside in the cold, and you keep doing this every
      winter.
    setting: community
    why: Putting the coat on prevents the cold, and the behavior maintains over time.
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A learner is told "no" after grabbing a toy and grabs less often afterward. Nothing
      was removed, so this is punishment rather than negative reinforcement.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      Work is taken away after screaming but the screaming stays exactly as frequent as
      before. Without an increase in behavior, no reinforcement has occurred.
    setting: home

The most common confusion in this whole vocabulary is reading "negative" as "bad." It is
an arithmetic sign: something was subtracted from the situation. A supervisor asking
whether a behavior is negatively reinforced is asking what the behavior makes go away.
