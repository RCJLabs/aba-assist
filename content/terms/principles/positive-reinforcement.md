---
id: positive-reinforcement
term: Positive Reinforcement
aliases: [SR+, reinforcement]
abbreviation: SR+
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    A contingency in which a stimulus is presented immediately following a response, and
    the future frequency of that response increases as a result. Whether a stimulus
    functions as a reinforcer is determined by its effect on behavior, not by assumption
    or by the learner's stated preference.
  plain: >-
    Something is added right after a behavior, and the person does that behavior more
    often from then on. Positive here means something was added. It does not mean nice.
  gloss: Adding something makes a behavior happen more
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner points at a cup, is handed water, and begins pointing at the cup far more
      often across the day.
    setting: home
    why: Water was delivered after pointing and pointing increased.
  - text: >-
      A staff member gets a laugh after telling a joke in a team meeting and tells more
      jokes in later meetings.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A learner is given a sticker they do not care about, and the behavior it followed
      stays at the same rate. A planned reward that does not change behavior is not a
      reinforcer.
    setting: school
  - text: >-
      Praise is delivered ten minutes after the behavior and nothing changes. Delay can
      break the contingency entirely.
    setting: clinic

Reinforcement is defined by its effect, which is why a "reinforcer" that does not change
behavior is not one. This is also why preference assessments exist: what a person picks
in a lineup is a reasonable guess, not proof.
