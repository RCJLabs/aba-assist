id: matching-law
term: Matching Law
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    The finding that when two or more behaviors are available at once, the proportion of
    responding devoted to each tends to match the proportion of reinforcement each
    produces. Rate, quality, immediacy and effort all count. It explains why a problem
    behavior that is reinforced sometimes competes so well with a replacement behavior
    that is reinforced less richly.
  plain: >-
    People split their behavior between options in line with how much each option pays
    off. If the problem behavior pays better, it wins.
  gloss: Behavior is allocated in proportion to reinforcement
examples:
  - text: >-
      A student's calling out gets an answer every time and hand raising gets one
      sometimes. Calling out stays the more common of the two.
    setting: school
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A single behavior on a single schedule with no alternative. Matching describes
      choice between options.
    setting: any
