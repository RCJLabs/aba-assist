id: extinction-burst
term: Extinction Burst
aliases: [burst, response burst]
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    A temporary increase in the frequency, intensity or variety of a behavior when the
    reinforcement that maintained it is first withheld. It is a predictable early
    effect of extinction, not a sign that the procedure is failing, and a plan that
    cannot be held through the burst should not be started, because giving in at the
    peak reinforces the more intense form.
  plain: >-
    When a behavior stops working, it often gets worse before it gets better. This is
    expected. Giving in at that point teaches the bigger version.
  gloss: Behavior briefly increases when reinforcement stops
examples:
  - text: >-
      Attention for whining is withheld as planned. For two days the whining is louder
      and longer than before, then it falls.
    setting: home
  - text: >-
      A student whose desk-tapping used to get a reaction taps harder and faster for the first
      three lessons after staff stop responding, before it drops away.
    setting: school
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A behavior that returns weeks after it had stopped. That later reappearance is
      spontaneous recovery.
    setting: home
