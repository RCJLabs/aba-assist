id: punishment
term: Punishment
aliases: [punisher, positive punishment, negative punishment]
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    A contingency in which a consequence follows a response and the future frequency of
    that response decreases. As with reinforcement, the term is defined strictly by its
    effect on behavior and carries no implication about intent, severity, or moral
    judgment.
  plain: >-
    Something happens right after a behavior, and the person does that behavior less
    often from then on. In this field the word says nothing about whether the consequence
    was harsh.
  gloss: A consequence that makes a behavior happen less
examples:
  - text: >-
      A person touches a hot pan, feels pain, and does not touch pans without checking
      again.
    setting: home
    why: The consequence followed the response and the response decreased.
  - text: >-
      Screen time ends when a game is thrown, and throwing drops sharply over the next
      two weeks.
    setting: home
    why: Something was removed and the behavior decreased.
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A supervisor reprimands a learner repeatedly and the behavior continues at the same
      rate. If behavior does not decrease, no punishment has occurred by this definition.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      Attention delivered after a behavior that then increases. That is reinforcement,
      even if the attention was a scolding.
    setting: school

Technicians do not select or design punishment procedures. Whether any reductive
procedure is used, and which one, is a decision made by the supervising analyst and
written into the plan, subject to the learner's rights and the organization's policy.
