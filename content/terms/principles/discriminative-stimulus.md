id: discriminative-stimulus
term: Discriminative Stimulus
aliases: [SD, S-Delta, stimulus control]
abbreviation: SD
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    A stimulus in the presence of which a particular response has been reinforced in the
    past, and which therefore comes to evoke that response. It signals the availability of
    reinforcement rather than altering its value. A stimulus correlated with the absence
    of reinforcement is called an S-delta.
  plain: >-
    Something in the room that tells a person a behavior will work right now. A green
    light tells you that driving forward will go fine. It does not change how badly you
    want to get there.
  gloss: A cue that says a behavior will work right now
examples:
  - text: >-
      A vending machine that is lit and stocked. Putting money in has paid off before when
      it looked like this, so a person does so again.
    setting: community
  - text: >-
      A learner asks for help only when a staff member is facing them, because requests
      made to someone turned away have not been answered.
    setting: clinic
    why: Staff orientation signals whether the request will work.
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Several hours without a drink, which makes water more valuable. That changes how
      much the learner wants water, not whether asking will succeed.
    setting: home
  - text: >-
      A stimulus a learner has never encountered before, which cannot yet signal anything
      about reinforcement.
    setting: any

Michael's 1982 paper exists precisely because these two ideas get mixed up. If you are
unsure which one you are looking at, ask whether the thing changed how much the learner
wanted the outcome, or only whether it was available.
