id: value-altering-effect
term: Value-Altering Effect
aliases: [value altering, reinforcer-establishing effect, reinforcer-abolishing effect]
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    The effect of a motivating operation on how well a stimulus works as a reinforcer or
    punisher. It is reinforcer-establishing when effectiveness goes up and
    reinforcer-abolishing when it goes down. This is the half of a motivating operation
    that is about worth, and it says nothing on its own about what the learner is doing.
  plain: >-
    How much a thing is worth to a person at this moment. The same drink is worth a lot
    after a run and very little just after one.
  gloss: How much the consequence is worth right now
examples:
  - text: >-
      An hour outdoors in the heat makes cold water a far stronger reinforcer than it was
      before the learner went out.
    setting: community
    why: Effectiveness went up, so this is the reinforcer-establishing form.
  - text: >-
      A learner has eaten a full lunch. For the next hour, food is a weak reinforcer and
      a token exchangeable for a snack buys very little effort.
    setting: school
    why: Effectiveness went down, so this is the reinforcer-abolishing form.
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A learner works harder when the adult holds up the token board. The board signals
      that tokens can be earned now; it does not change what a token is worth.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      A learner comes to like a previously neutral sound after it is paired with food
      many times. That is a learning history building a conditioned reinforcer, not a
      moment-to-moment change in worth.
    setting: clinic
contrastWith: [behavior-altering-effect]

Every motivating operation has both this effect and a behaviour-altering one, at the same
time. Splitting them is what makes the concept examinable: the exam can ask about the
worth of the consequence and about what the learner is doing separately, and the answer
is different for each.
