id: stimulus-control
term: Stimulus Control
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    The situation in which a behavior occurs more often in the presence of a particular
    stimulus than in its absence, because of a history of differential reinforcement in
    the presence of that stimulus. When stimulus control is established, the stimulus
    is a discriminative stimulus and the behavior is said to be under its control.
  plain: >-
    When a behavior happens more in the presence of a certain signal, because that
    is when it has paid off before. The signal now controls the behavior.
  gloss: A stimulus reliably changes the likelihood of a behavior
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner says "dog" when shown a picture of a dog and not when shown a cat. The
      picture controls the response.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A learner says "dog" to every animal picture. Responding is not yet under control
      of the relevant features.
    setting: clinic
