id: motivating-operation
term: Motivating Operation
aliases: [MO, establishing operation, abolishing operation, EO, AO]
abbreviation: MO
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    An environmental variable that alters the current effectiveness of a stimulus as a
    reinforcer or punisher, and correspondingly alters the frequency of behavior that has
    been reinforced by that stimulus. An establishing operation increases effectiveness;
    an abolishing operation decreases it.
  plain: >-
    Something that changes how much a person wants a thing right now, and so changes how
    hard they work for it. Going without food makes food worth more. Finishing a large
    meal makes it worth less.
  gloss: Changes how much a consequence is wanted right now
examples:
  - text: >-
      After an hour outside on a hot day, water becomes far more effective as a reinforcer
      and a learner asks for it repeatedly.
    setting: community
    why: Deprivation raised the value of water, so requesting increased.
  - text: >-
      A learner who just spent twenty minutes on a preferred tablet game shows little
      interest in earning tablet time during the next session.
    setting: clinic
    why: Recent access lowered the value of the item.
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A picture card showing that the tablet is available. The card signals when a request
      will work, not how much the tablet is wanted.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      A teacher walking into the room, which tells a learner that help is now available.
      That changes availability, not value.
    setting: school

The single most useful question this concept answers is why a reinforcer that worked
yesterday is doing nothing today. Often nothing is wrong with the plan; the learner is
simply satiated.
