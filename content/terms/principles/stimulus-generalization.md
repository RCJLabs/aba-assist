id: stimulus-generalization
term: Stimulus Generalization
category: principles
definition:
  technical: >-
    The spread of a behavior to stimuli that resemble the one present during training,
    without those stimuli having been trained directly. The more similar a new stimulus
    is to the trained one, the more likely the behavior. Too much of it is a
    discrimination problem; too little means the skill stays stuck to the teaching
    materials.
  plain: >-
    A behavior learned with one thing also happens with things that look or feel
    similar. Useful when it spreads to the right things, a problem when it spreads too
    far.
  gloss: A trained response spreads to similar stimuli
examples:
  - text: >-
      Taught to say "cup" to one blue cup, a learner says "cup" to a white mug at
      grandma's house.
    setting: home
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Taught to ask for help by raising a hand, a learner starts asking by saying
      "help." A new form of the response is response generalization.
    setting: school
