term: Confidentiality
aliases: [privacy, protected information, HIPAA]
category: ethics
definition:
  technical: >-
    The obligation to protect information about the people you serve, limiting disclosure
    to those with a legitimate need and to what law and employer policy permit. It covers
    names, images, recordings, written records, and details that could identify someone
    indirectly.
  plain: >-
    Keeping information about the people you work with private. This covers names, photos,
    notes, and any detail that could let someone work out who you mean.
  gloss: Protecting information about the people you serve
examples:
  - text: >-
      Discussing a learner only with the staff working with them, and only where the
      conversation cannot be overheard.
    setting: clinic
  - text: Storing written records where your employer's policy requires rather than in a personal bag.
    setting: community
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Posting about a session on social media without names, believing that removing the
      name makes it anonymous. Details can still identify a person.
    setting: any
  - text: >-
      Telling a family member about your day in a way that includes what a learner did and
      where.
    setting: home

The social-media case is the one that catches people out. A post with no name can still be
identifying when combined with where you work, when you posted, and who follows you.
