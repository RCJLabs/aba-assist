definition:
  technical: >-
    A learner's expressed willingness to take part in an activity, distinct from consent,
    which is the legally binding permission given by a person authorized to give it.
    Assent may be communicated vocally, through an alternative communication system, or
    through behavior, and its withdrawal is similarly expressed by behavior.
  plain: >-
    The learner showing that they are willing to take part. A parent can give legal
    permission, but the learner still tells you, in words or actions, whether they want to
    do this right now.
  gloss: The learner showing they are willing to take part

Assent has moved from the margins of this field to its center, and that shift is one of
the clearest differences between current practice and practice a decade ago. Noticing and
honoring assent withdrawal is a skill, and what your program does when a learner withdraws
assent should be written down rather than improvised.

If you are unsure whether a learner is assenting, that uncertainty is worth raising with
your supervisor. It is information about the program, not a failure on your part.
