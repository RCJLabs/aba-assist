id: reversal-design
term: Reversal Design
aliases: [withdrawal design, A-B-A-B design, ABAB design]
category: research-design
definition:
  technical: >-
    A single-case design in which treatment is introduced after baseline, withdrawn to
    see whether behavior returns toward baseline levels, and then reintroduced. Control
    is demonstrated when behavior tracks the presence and absence of treatment. It is
    unsuitable when the behavior cannot be reversed, such as a learned skill, or when
    withdrawing treatment would be unsafe or unethical.
  plain: >-
    Start treatment, stop it, start it again, and watch whether the behavior follows.
    Not used when stopping would be unsafe or the skill cannot be unlearned.
  gloss: Treatment introduced, withdrawn, and reintroduced
examples:
  - text: >-
      A noncontingent-attention procedure is run for a week, paused for a week, then
      resumed. Disruption falls, rises, and falls again.
    setting: school
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Treatment is introduced across three classrooms one at a time. That staggering is
      a multiple-baseline, not a reversal.
    setting: school
