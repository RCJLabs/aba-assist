id: momentary-time-sampling
term: Momentary Time Sampling
aliases: [MTS, time sampling]
abbreviation: MTS
category: measurement
definition:
  technical: >-
    A discontinuous measure in which an observation period is divided into intervals and
    the behavior is scored only if it is occurring at the moment each interval ends.
    Because it samples single instants, it neither consistently overestimates nor
    underestimates, and it frees the observer between samples.
  plain: >-
    You check at the end of each time block and mark whether the behavior is happening
    right at that moment. You are not watching the whole time, so you can do other things.
  gloss: Check only at the end of each interval
examples:
  - text: >-
      Every two minutes a teacher glances up and records whether the learner is working at
      that instant.
    setting: school
  - text: A timer sounds every minute and staff note whether the learner is engaged just then.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Scoring an interval because the behavior happened at any point within it, which is
      partial-interval recording.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      Watching continuously and counting every occurrence, which is a continuous measure
      rather than a sample.
    setting: school

This is the method most often chosen when one person has to teach and record at the same
time, because it asks for attention only at set moments.
