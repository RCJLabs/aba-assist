id: whole-interval-recording
term: Whole-Interval Recording
aliases: [WIR, whole interval]
abbreviation: WIR
category: measurement
definition:
  technical: >-
    A discontinuous measurement procedure in which an observation period is divided into
    equal intervals and the behavior is scored only if it occurs throughout the entire
    interval. Because any pause disqualifies the interval, it systematically
    underestimates the true occurrence of behavior.
  plain: >-
    You split the session into short blocks. You only mark a block if the behavior lasted
    the whole way through it. Any break means the block does not count, so this method
    tends to make behavior look less frequent than it is.
  gloss: Mark the interval only if the behavior lasted throughout
examples:
  - text: >-
      In one-minute intervals, a learner stays on task for the full minute. That interval
      is scored as on task.
    setting: school
  - text: >-
      Tracking sustained engagement with a group activity across a twenty-minute period
      in thirty-second intervals.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A learner works for forty seconds of a one-minute interval and then looks away. The
      interval is not scored, even though most of it was on task.
    setting: school
  - text: >-
      Scoring an interval because the behavior happened once within it. That is
      partial-interval recording.
    setting: clinic

Whole-interval recording is usually chosen for behavior you want to increase, such as
engagement, because underestimating means you will not conclude a skill is established
before it really is.
