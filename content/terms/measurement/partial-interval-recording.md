term: Partial-Interval Recording
aliases: [PIR, partial interval, discontinuous measurement]
abbreviation: PIR
category: measurement
definition:
  technical: >-
    A discontinuous measurement procedure in which an observation period is divided into
    equal intervals and the behavior is scored as occurring if it happens at any point
    during the interval, regardless of duration or number of occurrences. It systematically
    overestimates the true occurrence of behavior.
  plain: >-
    You split the session into short blocks. If the behavior happens at all during a
    block, you mark that block. One quick moment counts the same as the whole block, so
    this method tends to make behavior look more frequent than it is.
  gloss: Mark the interval if the behavior happened at all
examples:
  - text: >-
      In ten-second intervals, a learner shouts once at second three. That interval is
      scored, and the count moves on.
    setting: school
  - text: >-
      A plan tracks hand flapping in one-minute intervals across a thirty-minute session
      and reports the percentage of intervals scored.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Counting each separate instance of shouting as it happens. That is frequency
      recording, which is continuous rather than interval based.
    setting: school
  - text: >-
      Scoring an interval only when the behavior lasted the entire interval. That is
      whole-interval recording.
    setting: clinic

The bias is the point, not a flaw. Partial-interval recording is often chosen deliberately
for behavior you want to reduce, because overestimating means you are unlikely to
conclude that a problem has resolved when it has not.
