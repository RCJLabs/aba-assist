term: Frequency and Rate
aliases: [count, rate of responding, frequency count]
abbreviation: null
category: measurement
definition:
  technical: >-
    Frequency is a simple count of how many times a response occurs. Rate is that count
    divided by the time observed, which makes sessions of different lengths comparable.
    Both require a response with a clear beginning and end.
  plain: >-
    Frequency is how many times something happened. Rate is that number divided by how
    long you watched. Rate lets you compare a short session with a long one.
  gloss: How many times it happened, and how often per minute
examples:
  - text: A learner raised their hand nine times during a thirty-minute lesson, a rate of 0.3 per minute.
    setting: school
  - text: Staff recorded four requests for help across a two-hour session.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Recording how long a tantrum lasted. That is duration, because it measures time
      rather than how many times something happened.
    setting: home
  - text: >-
      Counting how often a learner stayed on task, which has no clear separate
      occurrences to count.
    setting: school

Rate is what a supervisor usually wants, because sessions are rarely the same length.
Reporting a raw count without the observation time makes the number hard to interpret.
