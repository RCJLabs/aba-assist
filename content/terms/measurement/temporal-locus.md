definition:
  technical: >-
    The property that every instance of behavior occurs at a point in time, and so stands
    in a temporal relation to other events. It is what latency and interresponse time
    measure — one against a preceding stimulus, the other against the previous response.
  plain: >-
    Behavior happens at a moment. So you can time when it happened, against some other
    event.
  gloss: Behavior occurs at a point in time, so it can be located

Temporal extent and temporal locus are the pair people mix up, because both are reported
in seconds. The test is what the seconds are measured between. Extent runs from the start
of an instance to its end. Locus runs from some other event to the instance.

That distinction decides which data sheet catches the problem. "He won't start" and "he
won't keep going" are different complaints, and only one of them shows up in a duration
total.
