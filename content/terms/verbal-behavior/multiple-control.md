id: multiple-control
term: Multiple Control
aliases: [convergent multiple control, divergent multiple control]
category: verbal-behavior
definition:
  technical: >-
    The rule that a verbal response usually has more than one controlling variable at
    once, and one variable usually affects more than one response. Convergent control
    means several variables combine to evoke one response; divergent control means one
    variable makes several responses more likely. Teaching that builds in multiple
    control produces more flexible language.
  plain: >-
    Most things we say are caused by several things at once, and one thing can bring
    up several possible words. Teaching with this in mind makes language more flexible.
  gloss: One response with several causes, or one cause with several responses
examples:
  - text: >-
      Asked "What animal says moo?", a learner answers "cow." The question, the word
      animal and the sound all combined to evoke it.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A learner who says "ball" only when a ball is present and only for that one
      picture. Control is narrow, not multiple.
    setting: clinic
