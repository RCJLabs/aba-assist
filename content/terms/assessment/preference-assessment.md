id: preference-assessment
term: Preference Assessment
aliases: [MSWO, paired stimulus, free operant preference assessment]
category: assessment
definition:
  technical: >-
    A structured procedure for identifying which items or activities a learner is likely
    to work for, by presenting options and recording what they approach or select.
    Preference varies over time and across conditions, so assessments are repeated rather
    than treated as a fixed list.
  plain: >-
    A set way of finding out what someone likes right now. You offer choices and record
    what they pick. What they like changes, so you check again.
  gloss: Finding out what a learner will work for right now
examples:
  - text: >-
      Several items are laid out, the learner chooses one, the chosen item is removed, and
      the remaining ones are offered again.
    setting: clinic
    why: Repeating without replacement produces a ranked order of preference.
  - text: >-
      A learner is left with free access to a set of toys while staff record what they go
      to and for how long.
    setting: home
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Asking a parent what a learner liked last year and using that list without checking.
      Report is a starting point, not an assessment.
    setting: any
  - text: >-
      Assuming that because an item is popular with other learners, this learner will work
      for it too.
    setting: clinic

A preference assessment predicts what is likely to reinforce; it does not prove it. Only
an effect on behavior does that, which is why the two terms are not interchangeable.
