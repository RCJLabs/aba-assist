---
id: chaining
term: Chaining
aliases: [forward chaining, backward chaining, total task]
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    Teaching the steps of a task analysis so they come to be performed as a connected
    sequence, in which completing each step both produces the next opportunity and
    signals it. Common arrangements teach from the first step forward, from the last step
    backward, or across every step each time.
  plain: >-
    Teaching the steps of a task so they join up into one smooth routine. You can start
    from the first step, from the last one, or work on all of them each time.
  gloss: Joining taught steps into one connected routine
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner is taught the final step of handwashing first, so every attempt ends with
      a completed task.
    setting: school
    why: Finishing the task is itself the natural consequence of the step just taught.
  - text: Staff support every step of making toast each time, fading help step by step.
    setting: home
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Gradually changing a single response until it looks like the target, which is
      shaping rather than linking separate steps.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      Teaching several unrelated skills in the same session. Chaining is about one
      sequence, not a set of goals.
    setting: any

Chaining depends on a task analysis that is already correct. If the steps are wrong or out
of order, no amount of teaching will make the sequence hold together.
