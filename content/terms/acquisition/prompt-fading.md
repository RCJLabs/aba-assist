term: Prompt Fading
aliases: [fading, most-to-least, least-to-most, transfer of stimulus control]
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    The systematic reduction of supplementary antecedent assistance so that responding
    transfers from the prompt to the naturally occurring discriminative stimulus. Common
    arrangements include moving from most intrusive to least intrusive assistance, the
    reverse, and progressive delay before delivering a prompt.
  plain: >-
    Slowly giving less help, so the person ends up responding to the real situation
    instead of to your help. The goal is that they do it without you there.
  gloss: Giving less help over time until none is needed
examples:
  - text: >-
      Hand-over-hand guidance is reduced to a touch at the elbow, then to a gesture, then
      to nothing at all across several sessions.
    setting: clinic
    why: Assistance is systematically thinned while the response stays intact.
  - text: >-
      A staff member waits two seconds before offering a model, then four seconds, giving
      the learner room to respond independently.
    setting: school
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Removing all help at once to see whether the learner can manage. That is a probe,
      not fading, and it risks a long run of errors.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      Continuing to give the same physical guidance every session for months. Without
      reduction there is no fading, and prompt dependence becomes likely.
    setting: home

Prompt dependence is the failure mode this guards against: a learner who performs
reliably with a particular person standing in a particular place, and not otherwise. The
prompt hierarchy a plan specifies is a clinical decision, so follow the one written for
your learner rather than the one you are used to.
