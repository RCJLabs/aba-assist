id: discrete-trial-training
term: Discrete Trial Training
aliases: [DTT, discrete trial teaching, massed trials]
abbreviation: DTT
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    A teaching format that breaks a skill into small units taught in repeated, clearly
    bounded trials, each with an instruction, an opportunity to respond, a consequence,
    and a brief pause before the next trial. It is adult-led and produces many practice
    opportunities in a short time.
  plain: >-
    Teaching in short, repeated tries. Each try has a clear start and end, and the adult
    decides what is taught and when. It packs a lot of practice into a short time.
  gloss: Short, repeated, adult-led teaching trials
examples:
  - text: >-
      A technician presents two cards, asks the learner to point to one, and records the
      response before pausing and starting the next trial.
    setting: clinic
  - text: Ten trials of a naming task run back to back at a table, with a short break between each.
    setting: school
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Waiting for a learner to reach for a toy and then teaching a request in that moment,
      which is learner-led rather than trial-based.
    setting: home
  - text: >-
      Practicing a skill once at the end of a session with no defined trial structure or
      recording.
    setting: any

The format is a tool, not a philosophy. How many trials, which skills, and how assent is
honored within them are decisions written into the plan by the supervising analyst.
