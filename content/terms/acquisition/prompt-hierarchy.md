id: prompt-hierarchy
term: Prompt Hierarchy
aliases: [prompt levels, levels of assistance]
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    An ordered set of prompts from least intrusive to most intrusive — typically
    verbal, gestural, model, partial physical, full physical, though the order is
    chosen for the learner and the skill. The hierarchy is the basis of both
    most-to-least and least-to-most prompting, and the plan should name which levels
    are in use.
  plain: >-
    A ranked list of kinds of help, from a small hint up to hand-over-hand. Plans use
    it to decide how much help to give and in what order.
  gloss: Prompts ranked from least to most intrusive
examples:
  - text: >-
      For putting on a coat: a verbal reminder, then a gesture toward the coat, then a
      demonstration, then guiding the arm.
    setting: home
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Giving the same full physical prompt every time regardless of what the learner
      does. With no ordering, there is no hierarchy in use.
    setting: home
