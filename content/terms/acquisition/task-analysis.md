---
id: task-analysis
term: Task Analysis
aliases: [TA, breaking down a skill]
abbreviation: TA
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    The process of breaking a complex skill into an ordered sequence of smaller, teachable
    steps, and the written sequence that results. The number of steps depends on the
    learner rather than on the task, so the same skill may need very different breakdowns.
  plain: >-
    Splitting a bigger skill into a list of small steps in order. How small the steps need
    to be depends on the person learning, not on the task.
  gloss: A skill broken into ordered, teachable steps
examples:
  - text: >-
      Hand washing written as eleven steps, from turning on the tap through to drying and
      turning it off.
    setting: school
  - text: >-
      Making a sandwich broken into steps, with more detail around the parts a learner
      finds hardest.
    setting: home
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A goal that simply says the learner will wash their hands, with no steps written
      down for staff to follow consistently.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      A list of unrelated skills to work on. A task analysis is one skill in order, not a
      collection of goals.
    setting: any

If several staff run the same programme slightly differently, the task analysis is usually
where to look first. Steps that read clearly to the person who wrote them can still be
ambiguous to everyone else.
