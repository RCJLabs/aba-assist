id: generalization
term: Generalization
aliases: [generalisation, stimulus generalization, response generalization, transfer]
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    The occurrence of a learned response under conditions other than those in which it was
    taught — with different people, settings, or materials — or the appearance of related
    responses that were never directly taught. It is planned for deliberately rather than
    assumed to follow from teaching.
  plain: >-
    Using a skill outside the exact situation where it was taught. With new people, in new
    places, or with different materials. It has to be planned for, not hoped for.
  gloss: Using a skill beyond where it was taught
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner who has been taught to ask for help at the table also asks a different
      staff member for help in the playground.
    setting: school
  - text: A skill taught with one set of cards is used correctly with unfamiliar objects.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      A skill that appears reliably with one person in one room and nowhere else. That is
      the absence of generalization.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      A skill still performed months later in the same setting, which is maintenance over
      time rather than transfer across conditions.
    setting: any

Stokes and Baer's point in 1977 still holds: generalization does not reliably happen by
itself. If a plan does not say how it will be programmed, that is worth asking about.
