id: backward-chaining
term: Backward Chaining
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    A chaining method in which the trainer completes all steps except the last, which
    the learner performs and is reinforced for; then the last two, and so on toward the
    beginning. Because the learner always completes the task and contacts the natural
    reinforcer at the end, it is often chosen for learners who need to see the finished
    result.
  plain: >-
    The helper does all the steps except the last one, which the learner does. Then the
    last two, working backward. The learner always gets to finish.
  gloss: Steps taught from the last one backward
examples:
  - text: >-
      Putting on a shirt: the technician does everything except pulling the hem down,
      which the learner does and gets praised for.
    setting: home
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      The learner attempts every step each time with help where needed. That is
      total-task chaining.
    setting: home
