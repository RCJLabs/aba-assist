id: naturalistic-teaching
term: Naturalistic Teaching
aliases: [NET, natural environment teaching]
abbreviation: NET
category: acquisition
definition:
  technical: >-
    Teaching that embeds learning opportunities in ongoing activities and follows the
    learner's current interest, using the naturally available consequence as the
    reinforcer. Opportunities arise as the activity unfolds rather than being scheduled in
    advance.
  plain: >-
    Teaching during normal play or daily routines. You start with what the learner
    already wants. The reward is that thing itself.
  gloss: Teaching inside everyday activities the learner chose
examples:
  - text: >-
      A learner reaches toward bubbles, and staff pause to give an opportunity to request
      before blowing them.
    setting: home
    why: The reinforcer is the bubbles themselves, which is what the learner wanted.
  - text: >-
      During snack, a learner is given a closed container so that asking for help becomes
      useful.
    setting: clinic
nonExamples:
  - text: >-
      Running ten scheduled trials at a table with a token as the consequence, which is a
      discrete-trial format.
    setting: clinic
  - text: >-
      Letting a session drift with no planned targets at all. Naturalistic teaching still
      has goals; it changes when and how they are taught.
    setting: any

Because the reinforcer is the natural outcome, motivation does much of the work here —
which is why this format is often easier to generalize beyond the teaching setting.
