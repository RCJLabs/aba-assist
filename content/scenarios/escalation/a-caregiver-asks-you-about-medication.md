id: a-caregiver-asks-you-about-medication
kind: escalation-only
title: A caregiver asks you whether a medication is working or should change
situation: >-
  A parent or caregiver asks your opinion about the learner's medication — whether it
  seems to be helping, whether the dose seems right, whether to skip it on session days,
  or whether a behavior you see could be a side effect. They ask because you see the
  learner often and they trust you.
setting: home
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: [medication-question]
escalation:
  stopAndEscalate: true
  contacts:
    - nurse-or-medical
    - supervising-bcba
    - parent-guardian
  immediateSafetyNote: >-
    Nothing you say here should be medical advice. Tell the caregiver that questions
    about medication are for the prescriber, offer to share your objective session data
    with the medical team through your supervisor, and never suggest starting, stopping,
    skipping or changing anything.
  mandatedReporterNote: null
  legalNote: >-
    Advising on medication is outside the scope of a behavior technician and of a
    behavior analyst. Objective behavior data can be genuinely useful to a prescriber,
    and sharing them goes through the supervising analyst and the consent process.
  documentation:
    - Record the question the caregiver asked and what you told them.
    - Record any behavior change the caregiver attributes to medication, as objective observations only.
    - Tell your supervisor the same day so that data can be shared with the medical team if the family wishes.
  consultYourPolicy: true
