title: You have been told to restrain or seclude a learner
situation: >-
  A supervisor, a teacher, a colleague or a family member tells you to restrain the
  learner, or to put them in a room on their own and keep the door shut. It is happening
  now, somebody senior is waiting for you to act, and refusing in front of everybody feels
  impossible.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: [restraint, seclusion]
escalation:
  stopAndEscalate: true
  contacts:
    - supervising-bcba
    - site-supervisor
    - agency-safety-officer
  immediateSafetyNote: >-
    Your first duty is that everyone in the room is safe, yourself included. If you have
    not completed your employer's certified training for restrictive procedures, and the
    learner's written plan does not authorise one, say out loud that you are not trained
    for this and get your supervisor on the phone. Not being trained is a reason, not an
    excuse, and saying so is the correct professional answer.
  mandatedReporterNote: null
  legalNote: >-
    Restrictive procedures are governed by state law, federal guidance and written employer
    policy together. In most places they are lawful only where there is imminent danger of
    serious physical harm, only by staff who hold a current certification, and only with
    documentation afterwards. That certification is assessed in person; no app can stand in
    for it.
  documentation:
    - Write down who asked you, exactly what was asked, and the time.
    - Write down what you said and who you contacted.
    - Write down what the learner's written plan authorises, if you have seen it.
    - Use your employer's incident form, and keep to what you observed rather than what you concluded.
    - Tell your supervising analyst the same day, in writing, even if they were the one who asked.
  consultYourPolicy: true
