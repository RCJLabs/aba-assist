title: You suspect a learner is being abused or neglected
situation: >-
  Something you have seen or heard makes you reasonably suspect that a learner is being
  physically, sexually or emotionally abused, or neglected — an unexplained injury, a
  disclosure, a pattern of fear, signs of not being fed or cared for. You are not
  certain, and you are not sure whether it is your place to say anything.

  - not being fed properly at home

  immediateSafetyNote: >-
    If the learner is in immediate danger, call emergency services. Otherwise make the
    report to the protective-services agency for your state yourself, today, and then
    tell your supervisor. Do not investigate, confront the suspected person, or promise
    the learner you will keep it secret.
  mandatedReporterNote: >-
    In most US states people who provide services to children or vulnerable adults are
    mandated reporters. The legal standard is reasonable suspicion, not proof, and the
    duty is personal: telling your supervisor or employer does not by itself satisfy it,
    and an employer may not stop you from reporting. Good-faith reports are protected.
  legalNote: >-
    Mandated-reporter duties, deadlines and hotlines are set by state law and differ by
    state. Your employer's policy will add steps but cannot remove the legal duty.
  documentation:
    - Write down exactly what you saw or heard, in the learner's words where there were words, with the date and time.
    - Record the time you made the report, the agency you reported to, and any reference number you were given.
    - Record when you informed your supervisor.
    - Keep your notes factual and free of conclusions; describe, do not interpret.

Reporting when you are unsure is what the law asks of you. Deciding whether the suspicion
is justified is the agency's job, and the consequences of a report that turns out to be
unfounded are far smaller than the consequences of silence.
