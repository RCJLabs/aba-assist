title: You think a colleague may be harming a client
situation: >-
  You see a colleague do something to a client you would not do — speaking to them
  cruelly, being rough with them, taking away something they need, leaving them distressed
  and walking off. Perhaps it was once. Perhaps you have noticed it building. They are
  senior to you, everyone else seems comfortable, and you are not certain.
askedAs:
  - rough with a client
  - speaks cruelly to a learner
  - saw staff mistreat a learner
  - someone senior is hurting a client
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: [suspected-abuse]
escalation:
  stopAndEscalate: true
  contacts:
    - supervising-bcba
    - child-protective-services
    - agency-safety-officer
    - site-supervisor
  immediateSafetyNote: >-
    See that the client is not left alone with the person you are worried about, and stay
    where you can see them. You are not the person who decides whether it happened, and
    you do not need proof — deciding is somebody else's job and waiting until you are sure
    is how these things continue.
  mandatedReporterNote: >-
    In most US states somebody working directly with a child or a vulnerable adult is a
    mandated reporter, and the duty is yours personally rather than your employer's. The
    standard is reasonable suspicion, not certainty. Telling your supervisor is not a
    substitute for a report unless your state's law says it is — check which, because the
    answer differs by state and the clock is usually short.
  legalNote: >-
    No employer may instruct you not to report, and no internal process replaces a legal
    duty. Both ethics codes require you to act on a concern about a client's welfare, and
    retaliation for reporting is separately unlawful in most jurisdictions. If your
    concern is about the person who supervises you, report above them.
  documentation:
    - Write down what you saw and heard, in the words you would use to describe it to somebody who was not there.
    - Record the date, the time, the setting and who else was present.
    - Keep your account to what happened rather than what you believe it means.
    - Record who you told, when, and what they said they would do.
    - Keep your own copy somewhere your employer does not control.
  consultYourPolicy: true
