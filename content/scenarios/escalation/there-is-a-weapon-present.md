title: There is a weapon in the room
situation: >-
  You see a weapon — in the home, in a car, in a bag, in a classroom, or in somebody's
  hand. It may belong to an adult who is not thinking about you at all. It may be a
  learner who has picked something up. You do not know, and you are deciding what to do in
  the next few seconds.
askedAs:
  - there is a gun in the house
  - picked up a knife
  - somebody has a weapon
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: [weapon]
escalation:
  stopAndEscalate: true
  contacts:
    - emergency-services-911
    - supervising-bcba
    - site-supervisor
    - parent-guardian
  immediateSafetyNote: >-
    Leave. Take whoever you can with you, put a door between you and it, and call
    emergency services from somewhere else. Do not try to take it, do not bargain for it,
    and do not stay to work out how serious it is — you are not equipped to make that
    assessment and being wrong once is the whole risk.
  mandatedReporterNote: null
  legalNote: >-
    Your employer's protocol and local law govern what happens next, and some jurisdictions
    and some funders require a report within a fixed number of hours. Leaving a session for
    your own safety is not abandoning a client; staying in a dangerous setting is not a
    duty anybody may place on you, and an instruction to stay does not make it one.
  documentation:
    - Write down what you saw, where it was, and the time.
    - Record who was present and what you did.
    - Record who you called and when.
    - Complete your employer's incident form the same day, before the detail fades.
  consultYourPolicy: true
