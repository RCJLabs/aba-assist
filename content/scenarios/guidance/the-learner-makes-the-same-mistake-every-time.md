id: the-learner-makes-the-same-mistake-every-time
kind: guidance
title: The learner keeps making the same error
situation: >-
  On one target the learner gets it wrong the same way every time. The error is
  consistent, and the correction procedure does not seem to be shifting it.
setting: clinic
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: >-
      Check that you are running the correction procedure the program specifies, in full,
      every time.
    rationale: >-
      Correction procedures are easy to shorten under time pressure, and a half-run
      correction can teach the error rather than the answer.
  - text: >-
      Look at what distinguishes the correct answer from the one the learner gives.
    rationale: >-
      A consistent error is usually a discrimination that has not been taught yet rather
      than a mistake being repeated.
  - text: >-
      Check whether the error gets a reaction. A wrong answer that produces attention or a
      break is being reinforced.
    rationale: >-
      What follows an error matters as much as what follows a correct response, and it is
      easier to overlook.
  - text: >-
      Record the error itself, not just that the trial was incorrect.
    rationale: >-
      Which wrong answer the learner gives is the information the supervisor needs. A column
      of zeroes says only that something is wrong.
  - text: >-
      Tell your supervisor, with the pattern.
    rationale: >-
      Changing the teaching arrangement — the materials, the prompts, the targets taught
      together — is a clinical decision.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not invent a new correction procedure.
  - >-
    Do not keep running the same trial repeatedly hoping it resolves.
  - >-
    Do not drop the target on your own initiative.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor when the same error persists across sessions.
  - >-
    Ask your supervisor if the program does not specify what to do after an error.
