title: A teacher tells you to do it differently in their classroom
situation: >-
  You are in a school. The teacher, or the classroom lead, tells you to stop doing what
  the plan says and do something else instead — ignore it, send the learner out, take the
  reinforcer away, or simply not run the program during their lesson. It is their room
  and they outrank you in it.
setting: school
audience: [RBT, BCaBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Comply immediately with anything about classroom safety or school rules, and treat the rest as a question to be resolved.
    rationale: >-
      A teacher is responsible for the room. That authority is real and is not in conflict
      with the plan on most of what they ask.
  - text: Say, without arguing, that the program was written by the supervising analyst and that you will get it sorted rather than change it yourself.
    rationale: >-
      It moves the disagreement off the two of you and onto the people whose decision it
      actually is, in front of a class where neither of you can talk it through.
  - text: Where the two genuinely conflict, do the least disruptive thing available and run nothing rather than run it differently.
    rationale: >-
      Not running a program loses a session of data. Running an altered version puts
      unusable data into the record and may teach the learner something nobody chose.
  - text: Tell your supervising analyst the same day, factually, including what the teacher's reason was.
    rationale: >-
      The reason usually matters more than the request. A teacher who says it disrupts the
      whole class is describing a real constraint the plan may need to accommodate.
  - text: Leave the conversation about how to reconcile them to the analyst and the school, and ask to be included when it happens.
    rationale: >-
      That conversation is a collaboration between professionals about a shared learner,
      and you are the person who will have to make whatever they agree actually work.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not modify the program on the teacher's instruction, however reasonable it sounds.
  - Do not tell the teacher they are wrong in front of the class, or in front of the learner.
  - Do not say nothing and quietly let the program lapse.
whenToEscalate:
  - Always report a conflict between the written plan and a classroom instruction to the supervising analyst.
  - >-
    If you are told to do something that would be restrictive, or that the learner's plan
    does not authorize, that is a different situation and stops here.
