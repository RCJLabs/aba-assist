title: You are asked to record hours you did not work
situation: >-
  Somebody asks you to write down time you did not work, or to record a session as
  longer or shorter than it was. It may be framed as rounding, as fixing a scheduling
  problem, or as something everybody does.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: >-
      Do not do it, and say so plainly at the time.
    rationale: >-
      A time record is the basis of a billing claim. Writing something you know is untrue is
      not an administrative shortcut.
  - text: >-
      Record what actually happened, exactly as it happened.
    rationale: >-
      Your own record is the thing you can defend later, and it is the only part of this you
      control.
  - text: >-
      Ask for the request in writing if it is being pressed.
    rationale: >-
      A request that will not survive being written down is telling you something about the
      request.
  - text: >-
      Report it to your supervisor, and to whoever your employer designates if the
      supervisor is the person asking.
    rationale: >-
      There is normally a route that does not run through the person making the request, and
      this is what it exists for.
  - text: >-
      Write down who asked, when, and what was said, and keep it somewhere you control.
    rationale: >-
      Your account of the conversation is worth far more written on the day than remembered
      months later.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not adjust the record and resolve to raise it later.
  - >-
    Do not accept that it is normal practice as a reason.
  - >-
    Do not sign a record somebody else has altered.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Report it to your supervisor the same day.
  - >-
    Use your employer's route for reporting concerns if the person asking is your
    supervisor.
