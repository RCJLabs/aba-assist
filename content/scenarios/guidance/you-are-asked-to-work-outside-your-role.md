id: you-are-asked-to-work-outside-your-role
kind: guidance
title: You are asked to do something that belongs to the analyst
situation: >-
  Somebody asks you to write a programme, run an assessment, decide a goal, change a
  criterion, attend a meeting and give a clinical opinion, or train another technician on
  a procedure. It is meant as a compliment, or it is meant as a favour, or the team is
  short-staffed and somebody has to.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Separate the parts you may do from the parts you may not, out loud.
    rationale: >-
      Most of these requests are mixed. Describing what you observed at a meeting is your
      job; saying what should be changed because of it is not, and naming the line is more
      useful than declining the whole thing.
  - text: Offer the version that is inside your role — the data, the observations, what you saw and when.
    rationale: >-
      It answers the actual need in most cases. Teams usually want the information rather
      than the decision.
  - text: Route the decision to the supervising analyst in front of whoever asked.
    rationale: >-
      It is not a refusal, it is a redirection, and doing it openly means nobody has to
      guess why you stepped back.
  - text: Where you are asked to train somebody, teach only what you were trained on and only with the analyst's agreement.
    rationale: >-
      Assisting with training is within the role. Deciding what training somebody needs,
      and signing that they are competent, is not.
  - text: Write down what was asked and what you did, once, without making a drama of it.
    rationale: >-
      Repeated requests are a pattern the analyst needs to see, and a plain contemporaneous
      note is what makes a pattern visible later.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not write or modify a programme, even a small change, even a sensible one.
  - Do not give an opinion about a diagnosis, a medication, or whether services should continue.
  - Do not sign anything attesting to somebody else's competence.
  - Do not accept the request because refusing feels rude — the responsibility for your work sits with your supervisor either way.
whenToEscalate:
  - Tell your supervising analyst about the request, whether or not you did any part of it.
  - >-
    If a supervisor is the person asking you to work outside your role, that goes above
    them, and both ethics codes expect it to.
