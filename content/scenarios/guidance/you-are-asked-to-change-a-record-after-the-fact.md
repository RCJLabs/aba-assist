id: you-are-asked-to-change-a-record-after-the-fact
kind: guidance
title: Somebody asks you to change a note or a time after the session
situation: >-
  A supervisor, a billing administrator or a colleague asks you to adjust something
  already submitted — round the session time up, move a session to a different date,
  soften a description, add a target that was not run, or sign a note somebody else wrote.
  It is presented as a formality.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Work out whether this is a correction or a change.
    rationale: >-
      Correcting a genuine error is normal and expected. Altering a record to say something
      that did not happen is a different act with a different name, and the two can look
      similar from the outside.
  - text: Make real corrections openly, as an amendment that shows the original and the reason.
    rationale: >-
      An amendment trail is what makes a corrected record trustworthy. A silently rewritten
      one is not, even when the new version is the accurate one.
  - text: Decline anything that would make the record say something untrue, and say why in one sentence.
    rationale: >-
      Session notes are clinical records and usually billing records at the same time.
      Inaccuracy in one is a funder matter and a legal matter, not only an internal one.
  - text: Never sign as though you delivered a session you did not, or wrote a note you did not write.
    rationale: >-
      A signature is an attestation. It is the specific thing an audit examines, and it is
      attached to your name and your certification rather than your employer's.
  - text: Put the request and your answer in writing, to yourself if to nobody else.
    rationale: >-
      If this ever comes back, the contemporaneous note is the whole of your defence.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not change a submitted record and say nothing about it.
  - Do not add data for trials that were not run, or times that were not worked.
  - Do not assume it is fine because everybody does it there.
whenToEscalate:
  - Tell your supervising analyst, and if the request came from them, take it above them.
  - >-
    A pattern of pressure to alter records is reportable to the certifying board, and both
    ethics codes treat documentation integrity as a core obligation rather than a
    procedural one.
