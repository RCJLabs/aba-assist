title: A caregiver asks you to change how a program is run
situation: >-
  A parent or teacher asks you, during or after a session, to do something differently:
  drop a target, add a reward, stop a procedure they dislike, or work on something new.
  They are reasonable and well-meaning, and they are asking you directly rather than the
  supervisor.
setting: home
audience: [RBT]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Listen fully and take the request seriously; write down exactly what was asked and why.
    rationale: >-
      Caregivers see things you do not, and their concerns are data the supervisor needs.
  - text: Explain that program changes are made by the supervising analyst, and that you will pass the request on today.
    rationale: >-
      Technicians implement plans; they do not modify them. Saying so plainly protects
      the caregiver's trust and your certification.
  - text: Continue running the program as written for the rest of the session unless something is unsafe.
    rationale: >-
      A change made by one person in one setting makes the data uninterpretable and the
      program inconsistent across staff.
  - text: Contact your supervisor the same day with the request and your notes.
    rationale: >-
      The caregiver deserves a prompt answer from the person who can give one.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not agree to the change to avoid an awkward conversation, and do not quietly do
    it "just this once."
  - >-
    Do not dismiss the request or argue the clinical case for the program; that
    conversation belongs to the supervisor.
  - >-
    Do not promise what the supervisor will decide.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Always: every request to change a program goes to the supervisor, the same day.
  - >-
    Sooner, by phone, if the caregiver says they will stop allowing the program or if
    the request is about a safety concern.
