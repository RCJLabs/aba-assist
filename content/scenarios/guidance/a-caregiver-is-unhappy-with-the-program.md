id: a-caregiver-is-unhappy-with-the-program
kind: guidance
title: A caregiver tells you the program is not working
situation: >-
  A parent says the program is not helping, or is upsetting their child, or is not what
  they agreed to. They may be frustrated with you personally. They may be right. You are
  the person in the room and you did not write the plan.
setting: home
audience: [RBT, BCaBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Listen properly first, and do not defend the plan while they are still explaining what is wrong with it.
    rationale: >-
      A caregiver raising a concern is giving the team information nobody else has. Treating
      it as something to be managed is how it stops being offered.
  - text: Say back what you heard, specifically enough that they can correct you.
    rationale: >-
      It both checks your understanding and shows that the concern is being carried rather
      than absorbed, which is usually what the person actually wants.
  - text: Be clear about what you can and cannot do — that you will pass it on today, and that changes to the plan are the analyst's.
    rationale: >-
      Promising a change you cannot make is worse than saying no, because the next session
      arrives unchanged and the family concludes that raising things is pointless.
  - text: Keep running the plan as written until you are told otherwise.
    rationale: >-
      A plan the family has objected to is a plan under review, not a plan suspended. Only
      the analyst, with the family, can suspend it.
  - text: Report it the same day, in the caregiver's own terms rather than your summary of them.
    rationale: >-
      The analyst needs to hear the concern, not a version of it that has already been
      softened for them.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not agree that the plan is wrong, even if you think so, and do not defend it as though it could not be.
  - Do not offer a modification to keep the peace in the moment.
  - Do not treat the complaint as being about you, or wait until the next supervision to mention it.
whenToEscalate:
  - Always pass on a caregiver's concern about the program the same day.
  - >-
    A caregiver withdrawing agreement to a procedure, or asking for services to stop, goes
    to the analyst immediately — consent is theirs to withdraw and it is not yours to
    negotiate.
