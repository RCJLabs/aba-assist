id: learner-pushes-materials-away
kind: guidance
title: A learner pushes the materials away when you present a task
situation: >-
  You present a task the learner has done before. They push the materials across the
  table, turn away, and do not respond to the instruction. Nobody is at risk of harm and
  nothing has been damaged. You have a written program for this learner and this task.
setting: clinic
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Keep your own voice, face, and movements calm and ordinary.
    rationale: >-
      Your reaction is part of the situation. A large response can become the outcome that
      maintains the behavior.
  - text: Check what the learner's written program says to do at this point, and do that.
    rationale: >-
      The program was written by the supervising analyst with knowledge of this learner's
      assessment. Consistency across staff is most of what makes it work.
  - text: >-
      Notice what happened immediately before and immediately after, and write it down
      while it is fresh.
    rationale: >-
      Antecedent and consequence information is what your supervisor needs to tell whether
      the program still fits.
  - text: >-
      Carry on with the session as your program directs, rather than improvising a new
      consequence.
    rationale: >-
      Changing the plan mid-session makes the data uninterpretable and is outside a
      technician's scope.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not invent a new consequence, reward, or removal that is not written into the
    program, however reasonable it seems in the moment.
  - >-
    Do not decide on your own that the task is too hard and drop it, which may accidentally
    reinforce pushing the materials away.
  - >-
    Do not interpret what the behavior means and change the program accordingly. Assessment
    and program design belong to the supervising analyst.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor the same day if this is new, if it is getting more frequent, or if
    the program does not appear to cover what happened.
  - >-
    Contact your supervisor before the next session if you are not confident you know what
    the program asks you to do here.

This is the most common single situation a new technician asks about, and the honest
answer is usually unsatisfying: follow the plan, record what happened, and tell your
supervisor. The plan is where the clinical thinking lives.
