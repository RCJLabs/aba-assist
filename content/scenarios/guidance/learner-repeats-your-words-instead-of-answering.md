kind: guidance
title: The learner repeats your question instead of answering it
situation: >-
  You ask "What do you want?" and the learner says "What do you want?" You offer a
  choice — "cracker or apple?" — and the learner says "cracker or apple." The learner has
  echoic skills and is being taught to answer questions and to request.
setting: clinic
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Treat the repetition as information about the prompt, not as a wrong answer to be corrected.
    rationale: >-
      A learner who echoes is responding to the last thing they heard. The teaching
      problem is transferring control from your words to the question or the item.
  - text: Use the prompting and transfer procedure the program specifies — often a shorter prompt, a pause, or presenting the item before the words.
    rationale: >-
      Programs for this exact situation usually fade the verbal model so the question or
      the item comes to control the answer.
  - text: Reinforce the response the program targets, and record whether it was echoed, prompted or independent.
    rationale: >-
      The prompt-level data show whether transfer is happening.
  - text: Avoid offering choices as a question when the program says to present items instead.
    rationale: >-
      A spoken list of options is itself a model to echo.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not say "no" or repeat the question louder; both add more words to echo.
  - >-
    Do not stop asking questions altogether.
  - >-
    Do not decide the learner is being difficult; echoing is a skill, and this is the
    stage where it is being built on.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor if echoing is blocking progress across programs or if the
    program has no transfer procedure written for it.
