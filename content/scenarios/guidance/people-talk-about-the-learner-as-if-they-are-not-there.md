id: people-talk-about-the-learner-as-if-they-are-not-there
kind: guidance
title: People discuss the learner in front of them
situation: >-
  A caregiver, a teacher, or a colleague starts talking about the learner while the
  learner is present — what they cannot do, what happened last week, what is difficult
  about them. The learner can hear it.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: >-
      Assume the learner understands what is being said, whatever their communication looks
      like.
    rationale: >-
      Understanding and speaking are different abilities. A person who does not speak may
      follow the conversation completely.
  - text: >-
      Move the conversation if you can — suggest continuing it later, or step away from
      where the learner is.
    rationale: >-
      Changing when and where the conversation happens costs almost nothing and does not
      require correcting anybody.
  - text: >-
      Speak about the learner as you would if they were part of the conversation, because
      they are.
    rationale: >-
      The words a room uses about somebody in front of them shape how everybody in that room
      treats them, including the person themselves.
  - text: >-
      Bring the learner into it where you can, at whatever level they can take part.
    rationale: >-
      Being talked about and being talked to are different experiences, and the second is
      usually available.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not join in with a list of what the learner cannot do.
  - >-
    Do not discuss the learner's history or diagnosis with anybody who does not need it.
  - >-
    Do not tell the learner it does not matter or that they were not listening.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Raise it with your supervisor if it happens often in a setting, so it can be addressed
    with the team rather than in the moment.
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor if what is being said in front of the learner is distressing
    them.
