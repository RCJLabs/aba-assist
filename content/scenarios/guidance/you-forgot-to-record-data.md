title: You realize you did not record data for part of the session
situation: >-
  Halfway through writing up the session you realize a block of trials, or a stretch of
  time, was never recorded. You remember roughly how it went. The note and data sheet are
  due today.
setting: clinic
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Record only what you actually observed and recorded at the time, and mark the gap clearly as missing.
    rationale: >-
      Data reconstructed from memory look identical to real data on a graph and are not.
      A visible gap is honest and interpretable; a filled-in gap is neither.
  - text: Write a brief, factual note about the gap — what was not recorded, for how long, and why.
    rationale: >-
      The supervisor needs to know how much of the session's data to trust.
  - text: Tell your supervisor the same day rather than hoping it goes unnoticed.
    rationale: >-
      Missing data are a normal problem with a normal fix. Concealed data are an
      integrity problem.
  - text: Work out what made recording fail — sheet layout, timing, too many targets — and suggest a fix.
    rationale: >-
      Recording that keeps failing is a measurement-system problem, not a character
      flaw, and supervisors can change the system.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not estimate numbers and write them as if they were recorded.
  - >-
    Do not copy the previous session's data as a stand-in.
  - >-
    Do not leave the gap unexplained and let it be read as zero occurrences.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Always: any gap or error in clinical data is reported to the supervisor the same
    day.
