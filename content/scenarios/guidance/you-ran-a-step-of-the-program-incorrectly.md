title: You realize you have been running part of a program wrong
situation: >-
  You discover — from re-reading the plan, from a colleague, or on your own — that you
  have been doing one step differently from how it is written. Perhaps you prompted too
  soon, reinforced a response the plan says not to, or used the wrong interval. It may
  have been going on for a while.
setting: clinic
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Correct it from the next trial onward, exactly as written.
    rationale: >-
      The plan as written is the intervention. Continuing the error while you decide
      what to say adds more affected data.
  - text: Tell your supervisor the same day what was done differently, for how long, and in which sessions.
    rationale: >-
      Data collected under a different procedure mean something different. The supervisor
      needs to know which sessions to interpret with care.
  - text: Ask for a brief check on the step if you are not certain you now have it right.
    rationale: >-
      Watching someone do it and trying it once with feedback is how procedures are
      actually learned.
  - text: Note the error and the date corrected in the session record, factually.
    rationale: >-
      An honest record protects the learner, the supervisor, and you.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not quietly fix it and say nothing; the data from the affected sessions are still
    in the graph.
  - >-
    Do not go back and change earlier data to match what should have happened.
  - >-
    Do not assume the error did no harm because the learner seemed fine.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Always, the same day. A procedure run differently is exactly what treatment
    integrity measurement exists to catch, and reporting it yourself is the right
    outcome.
