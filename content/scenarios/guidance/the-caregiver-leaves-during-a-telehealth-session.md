id: the-caregiver-leaves-during-a-telehealth-session
kind: guidance
title: The caregiver walks away during a telehealth session
situation: >-
  You are running a session over video. The adult who was meant to stay has gone to
  another room, or left the house, and the learner is now alone in front of the camera
  with you. Everything is calm. It is also not the arrangement that was agreed.
setting: telehealth
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Ask for the caregiver to come back, by name, before continuing.
    rationale: >-
      A supervising adult present is usually a condition of the service agreement rather
      than a courtesy, and the request is easier made straight away than after twenty
      minutes have passed.
  - text: If nobody returns, stop the teaching and stay on the call while you reach an adult.
    rationale: >-
      Ending the call leaves the learner alone with no adult aware of it. Staying present
      and unengaged is almost always the safer of the two.
  - text: Reach the caregiver by phone, then your supervisor, then whoever the service agreement names.
    rationale: >-
      Working down a known list is faster than deciding in the moment, and it is the part of
      this worth agreeing before it ever happens.
  - text: Record the session as not delivered as agreed, with the times.
    rationale: >-
      It matters clinically and it matters for billing, and the two sets of records have to
      say the same thing.
  - text: Ask for the arrangement to be written down again with the family, rather than treating it as a one-off.
    rationale: >-
      Once it has happened it will happen again. A clear shared expectation is kinder than
      repeating the conversation every few weeks.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not keep teaching and hope somebody comes back.
  - Do not end the call and assume an adult is nearby.
  - Do not record the session as delivered normally.
whenToEscalate:
  - Tell your supervisor the same day, even where nothing went wrong.
  - >-
    If you cannot reach any adult at all, or you see something that worries you about the
    learner being unsupervised, that stops being a scheduling matter and goes straight up.
