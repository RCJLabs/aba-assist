kind: guidance
title: Your data do not match the other observer
situation: >-
  Two of you recorded the same session and the numbers are different — sometimes very
  different. One of you has counted something the other did not, or you have drawn the
  line between behaviours in different places.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: >-
      Do not change your data to match. Report both records as they are.
    rationale: >-
      Two independent records are the whole point of the exercise. Adjusting one to fit the
      other destroys the only information it carried.
  - text: >-
      Compare the written definition together, and find the case where you disagreed.
    rationale: >-
      Disagreement is nearly always about the definition rather than about attention.
      Finding the specific case is what fixes it.
  - text: >-
      Check whether you were both using the same measure over the same interval.
    rationale: >-
      Counting occurrences and sampling intervals produce different numbers from the same
      session, and so do different start times.
  - text: >-
      Report the disagreement to your supervisor rather than settling it between yourselves.
    rationale: >-
      The definition is part of the programme, and rewriting it is the supervisor's to do.
  - text: >-
      Keep taking data the way the definition says until you are told otherwise.
    rationale: >-
      Changing method partway through leaves a record where the numbers are not comparable
      across the change.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not adjust your figures towards the other observer's.
  - >-
    Do not agree a private working definition between you.
  - >-
    Do not discard the session's data.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor whenever an agreement check comes out low.
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor if you do not understand a definition well enough to apply it
    consistently.
