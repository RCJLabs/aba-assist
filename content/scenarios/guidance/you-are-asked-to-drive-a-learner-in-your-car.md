title: Somebody asks you to drive the learner in your own car
situation: >-
  A caregiver, a colleague, or the learner themselves asks you to give the learner a ride
  in your own vehicle. It would be convenient, and the reason sounds sensible.
setting: community
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Say that you need to check your employer's policy before agreeing, and do not agree in the moment.
    rationale: >-
      Transport is governed by employer policy, insurance and consent, none of which a
      technician can resolve standing in a doorway.
  - text: Ask your supervisor what the policy is, and get the answer in writing.
    rationale: >-
      A verbal yes from somebody who is not the decision-maker protects nobody, least of all
      you.
  - text: If the answer is no, say so plainly and without apology, and offer what you can do instead.
    rationale: >-
      Declining a request that falls outside your role is doing the job correctly, not
      refusing to help.
  - text: If the answer is yes, follow the conditions attached to it exactly.
    rationale: >-
      Permission usually comes with conditions — consent on file, specific insurance, a second
      adult — and the conditions are the permission.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not agree because it is only this once, or because it is a short distance.
  - >-
    Do not let a caregiver's assurance stand in for your employer's policy.
  - >-
    Do not treat the learner's own request as the decision.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Ask your supervisor before agreeing, every time, even if you have been told yes before
    for a different learner.
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor if you are being asked repeatedly after saying no.
