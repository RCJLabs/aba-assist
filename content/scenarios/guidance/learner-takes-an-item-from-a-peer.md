id: learner-takes-an-item-from-a-peer
kind: guidance
title: The learner takes a toy or material from another child
situation: >-
  During a group activity the learner reaches over and takes an item another child is
  using. The other child protests. Nobody has been hurt and nobody is at risk of being
  hurt; it is an ordinary conflict over materials.
setting: school
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Respond the way the learner's plan says, if the plan covers taking items; if it does not, respond the way the classroom's usual routine does.
    rationale: >-
      Peer conflict is common enough that many plans address it. When they do not,
      consistency with the setting matters more than improvising something clever.
  - text: Prompt the replacement behavior the learner has been taught — asking, waiting, or offering a trade — and reinforce it when it happens.
    rationale: >-
      Taking items usually serves a clear function. The plan's replacement behavior is
      the alternative that gets the same result acceptably.
  - text: Attend to the child who lost the item as the setting expects, without making a large event of it.
    rationale: >-
      Both children are watching what taking an item produces. Big reactions can become
      the reinforcer.
  - text: Record the incident and what came before it.
    rationale: >-
      If taking items is increasing, the supervisor will need the pattern to decide
      whether to add a program.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not lecture the learner or demand an apology unless that is in the plan.
  - >-
    Do not remove the learner from the group as an improvised consequence.
  - >-
    Do not ignore the other child's protest; they are entitled to a fair response.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor if this is frequent, if it is new, or if the learner's plan has
    no procedure for it.
