title: A family offers you a gift, a meal, or a favor
situation: >-
  A caregiver hands you a wrapped present, invites you to a birthday party, offers to
  pay you for extra hours outside the agency, or asks you to babysit. They mean it
  kindly, and refusing feels rude.
setting: home
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Know your employer's policy and the ethics code's limits on gifts before it happens, so the answer is ready.
    rationale: >-
      Both codes address gifts and multiple relationships. The rules exist to protect
      the client relationship, and knowing them lets you decline without fumbling.
  - text: Thank the person warmly, and explain that the professional rules you work under limit what you can accept.
    rationale: >-
      Attributing the refusal to the rules keeps it from feeling like a rejection of
      the person.
  - text: Decline private paid work, babysitting, and social invitations that would create a second relationship with the family.
    rationale: >-
      A second relationship makes objective judgment about services harder and creates
      real conflicts of interest.
  - text: Tell your supervisor what was offered and how you responded.
    rationale: >-
      Supervisors need to know about offers that could become recurring pressure, and
      they can follow up with the family themselves.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not accept something and decide later whether it was allowed.
  - >-
    Do not accept a gift because it was small "this time"; small gifts become expected
    ones.
  - >-
    Do not take paid work with a client's family outside your employer's arrangement.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor the same day about any offer of money, private work, or a
    gift you could not politely refuse in the moment.
