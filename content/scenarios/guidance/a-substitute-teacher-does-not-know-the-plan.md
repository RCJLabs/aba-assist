id: a-substitute-teacher-does-not-know-the-plan
kind: guidance
title: There is a substitute teacher who does not know the learner
situation: >-
  You arrive at the classroom and the usual teacher is absent. The substitute does not
  know the learner, has not seen the plan, and is running the room differently. The
  learner is noticing.
setting: school
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: >-
      Introduce yourself and say what you are there to do, in one or two sentences.
    rationale: >-
      A substitute who does not know why an extra adult is working with one child may
      reasonably treat you as an interruption.
  - text: >-
      Keep running the plan as written. It does not change because the teacher did.
    rationale: >-
      The plan is the learner's, not the classroom's. A day off it is a day of different
      data and a different experience for the learner.
  - text: >-
      Share only what the substitute needs to support the session, and nothing more about
      the learner.
    rationale: >-
      The substitute is entitled to what makes the room work today. They are not
      automatically entitled to the learner's history.
  - text: >-
      Expect the day to be harder than usual, and record it.
    rationale: >-
      A changed routine is a setting event. Data from a day like this should be readable as
      such later, rather than looking like a sudden loss of skill.
  - text: >-
      Tell the substitute who to contact if something comes up that you cannot handle.
    rationale: >-
      You are not the person who runs the classroom, and in an unfamiliar room it is worth
      saying so early.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not take over running the classroom.
  - >-
    Do not hand over the behaviour plan or any document about the learner without checking
    who may see it.
  - >-
    Do not drop the programme for the day because the room is unsettled.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor the same day if you could not run the session as written.
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor if substitute days are frequent, because the plan may need to
    account for them.
