title: Someone outside work asks you about a client
situation: >-
  A friend, relative, neighbor or acquaintance mentions a family you work with and asks
  a question — whether you work with them, how the child is doing, what is wrong with
  them. They may already know or guess that you provide services to the family.
setting: community
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Do not confirm or deny that the person is a client, even if the asker seems to know.
    rationale: >-
      Confirming a service relationship is itself a disclosure. The safest, kindest
      answer is that you cannot discuss anyone you may or may not work with.
  - text: Say plainly that confidentiality rules stop you from talking about anyone's services, and change the subject.
    rationale: >-
      A clear, friendly refusal is easier for everyone than an evasive one.
  - text: If the person raises a safety concern about a child, tell them how to contact protective services or the school directly.
    rationale: >-
      Concerns about a child's safety are for the authorities, not for you to relay
      informally.
  - text: Let your supervisor know it happened, especially if it is likely to recur.
    rationale: >-
      Small-community overlap is a known risk, and supervisors can help plan for it.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not share anything, including "they're doing great." Positive information is still
    information.
  - >-
    Do not ask the person to keep something secret; that means you have already
    disclosed it.
  - >-
    Do not post or message about work situations, even without names.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor the same day if you think you disclosed something, or if the
    person asking may have a relationship with the family that creates a conflict.
