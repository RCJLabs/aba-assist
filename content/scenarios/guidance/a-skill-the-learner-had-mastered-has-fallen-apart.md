id: a-skill-the-learner-had-mastered-has-fallen-apart
kind: guidance
title: A skill the learner had mastered is no longer there
situation: >-
  A target that was met weeks ago has come apart. The learner is getting it wrong, or
  not responding at all, on something the data say they mastered.
setting: clinic
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: >-
      Check that you are running it the way it was run when it was mastered, including the
      instruction and the materials.
    rationale: >-
      The most common reason a mastered skill fails is that something about how it is asked
      for has drifted.
  - text: >-
      Check what has changed around the learner — people, setting, schedule, health, sleep.
    rationale: >-
      Conditions outside the session change how much of what somebody knows is available on
      a given day.
  - text: >-
      Check what maintains it now. A skill that no longer produces anything tends to stop.
    rationale: >-
      Reinforcement is usually thinned deliberately after mastery, and thinning too far
      looks exactly like losing a skill.
  - text: >-
      Run the maintenance procedure the plan specifies rather than reteaching from the
      start.
    rationale: >-
      Reteaching a skill somebody still partly has wastes session time and can make the
      program look like it is going backwards.
  - text: >-
      Tell your supervisor with dates and numbers.
    rationale: >-
      Whether this is drift, thinning, or something outside the session is a judgment the
      supervisor makes from the record.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not reteach from the beginning on your own initiative.
  - >-
    Do not increase the reinforcement schedule yourself.
  - >-
    Do not remove the target from the maintenance list.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor when a mastered target fails across more than one session.
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor the same day if several mastered skills have come apart at once.
