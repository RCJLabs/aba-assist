id: you-think-the-plan-is-not-working
kind: guidance
title: You think the plan is not working
situation: >-
  You have run it as written for weeks. The learner is not progressing, or is getting more
  distressed, or the procedure seems to fit a different child than the one in front of you.
  You are the person who sees them most and nobody has asked your view.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Turn the impression into something countable before you raise it.
    rationale: >-
      "It is not working" invites a discussion about opinions. Twelve sessions of flat data
      and three consecutive sessions ending early is a finding, and it is one an analyst can
      act on immediately.
  - text: Check your own delivery honestly against how the procedure is written.
    rationale: >-
      Drift is normal and invisible from the inside. A plan being run slightly differently
      each week is the single commonest reason a good plan produces bad data, and it is much
      easier to fix than a plan revision.
  - text: Bring the data, your description of what you are seeing, and anything about the setting that has changed.
    rationale: >-
      Illness, sleep, a new sibling, a substitute teacher — the things outside the programme
      are often the answer, and you are the person positioned to notice them.
  - text: Raise it as a question rather than a conclusion, and say what you would want to look at.
    rationale: >-
      It is genuinely a question. You are seeing one part of a case, and the analyst may be
      holding constraints — funder, consent, a longer timeline — you have not been told about.
  - text: Keep running it as written while it is being considered.
    rationale: >-
      Changing delivery midway destroys the comparison that would show whether it needed
      changing.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not adjust the procedure yourself to see whether that helps.
  - Do not raise it in front of the family, which puts them in the middle of a clinical disagreement.
  - Do not stay silent about it because you are not the one who writes plans.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Raise it in the next supervision contact at the latest, and sooner in writing if the
    learner is becoming more distressed.
  - >-
    Anything where the learner is being harmed, or is refusing in a way that looks like
    withdrawn assent, goes to the analyst immediately rather than waiting for a scheduled
    contact.
