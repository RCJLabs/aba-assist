title: The month is nearly over and you have had no supervision
situation: >-
  It is the twenty-sixth. Looking back at the calendar, none of the contacts that were
  meant to happen this month did — they were canceled, or rescheduled into nothing, or
  simply never arranged. You have been delivering services the whole time.
setting: any
audience: [RBT, BCaBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Work out where you actually stand before you raise it — hours delivered this month, contacts that did happen, and which requirements are unmet.
    rationale: >-
      The requirement is a percentage of the hours you delivered, counted per calendar
      month and per organization. A specific number is a conversation; a vague worry is not.
  - text: Ask your supervisor in writing for the remaining contacts before the month ends, naming the dates you are available.
    rationale: >-
      A calendar month does not extend, and a written request is the record that you asked
      in time.
  - text: If your organization has a requirements coordinator, copy them in.
    rationale: >-
      Making sure the requirements are met is specifically their job, and they can schedule
      across supervisors in a way you cannot.
  - text: Keep delivering services only while a qualified supervisor is on record for you.
    rationale: >-
      Supervision falling short in a month is a problem to fix. Having nobody on record at
      all is a different thing — it makes the certification inactive.
  - text: Record the shortfall honestly rather than rounding it away, and expect to declare it at recertification.
    rationale: >-
      The recertification application asks directly whether you met the requirement. A
      truthful answer with a documented reason is survivable; an untruthful one is not.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not backdate a contact, stretch one meeting across two months, or count a phone call that was not a real-time supervision contact.
  - Do not count a professional development event as supervision — one activity cannot serve both requirements.
  - Do not stay quiet because raising it feels like complaining about your supervisor.
whenToEscalate:
  - If the month closes short, tell your supervisor and your employer in writing and keep the record.
  - >-
    If it happens repeatedly, or you are asked to record supervision that did not happen,
    that is an ethics matter and goes above the person asking.
