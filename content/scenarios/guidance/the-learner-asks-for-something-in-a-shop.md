kind: guidance
title: The learner asks for something you are not going to buy
situation: >-
  You are in a shop with the learner. They ask for an item, and the answer is no — it is
  not on the list, not in the budget, or not something the family allows. They ask again,
  louder, and then keep asking.
setting: community
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Answer once, clearly, and in the way the plan specifies if it covers this.
    rationale: >-
      Repeated answers to a repeated request can become the attention that keeps the request
      going.
  - text: Tell the learner what they can have or do instead, if the plan allows an alternative.
    rationale: >-
      An answer that offers something is easier to accept than one that only takes something
      away.
  - text: Expect the asking to get louder or longer before it stops, and keep your answer the same.
    rationale: >-
      A request that used to work usually gets more intense before it fades. Giving in at that
      point teaches that intensity is what works.
  - text: Reinforce the asking you want to see, in the form the learner has been taught, as soon as it appears.
    rationale: >-
      Asking is not the problem; the plan usually wants more of it, not less. What is being
      changed is what it produces.
  - text: Record what happened so the pattern is visible.
    rationale: >-
      Whether shops stay in the programme is decided from data, not from how the trip felt.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not buy the item to end the episode.
  - >-
    Do not negotiate, or explain at length why the answer is no.
  - >-
    Do not add a consequence the plan has not authorised.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor if this happens on most trips, or if the plan has no procedure for
    it.
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor the same day if anything happened that the plan did not anticipate.
