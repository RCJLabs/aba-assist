kind: guidance
title: A stranger stares, comments, or offers advice in public
situation: >-
  You are out in the community with the learner. They are doing something other people
  notice — being loud, moving in a way that draws looks, or becoming upset. A stranger
  stares, says something to you, or offers advice. Nobody is at risk.
setting: community
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Keep running the session. Your attention belongs with the learner, not with the stranger.
    rationale: >-
      The outing is the teaching opportunity. Turning to manage a bystander hands the session
      over to somebody who is not part of it.
  - text: Say as little as possible, and nothing that identifies the learner as somebody you work with.
    rationale: >-
      Confirming that this is a client, or that you are their technician, discloses a
      relationship the family has not agreed to make public.
  - text: If you need to say something, keep it short and general, and return your attention to the learner.
    rationale: >-
      A brief, neutral reply usually ends the exchange faster than an explanation does.
  - text: Move to a quieter part of the space if the plan allows it and it would help the learner.
    rationale: >-
      Changing the surroundings is an antecedent change, and it is often within what the plan
      already permits.
  - text: Record what happened if the outing was a programmed activity.
    rationale: >-
      Whether the setting can be used again is a decision the supervisor makes from what
      actually happens there.
whatNotToDo:
  - >-
    Do not describe the learner's diagnosis, program, or history to anybody who asks.
  - >-
    Do not argue with the stranger or try to correct their opinion. You are working.
  - >-
    Do not apologize for the learner as though they have done something wrong.
whenToEscalate:
  - >-
    Tell your supervisor the same day if anybody photographed or filmed the learner, or if
    the family is likely to hear about this from somebody else first.
  - >-
    Raise it with your supervisor if outings to this place keep going this way, so the plan
    can account for it.
