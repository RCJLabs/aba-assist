id: a-caregiver-wants-to-connect-with-you-privately
kind: guidance
title: A caregiver asks to add you on social media or hire you privately
situation: >-
  A parent sends you a friend request, asks for your personal number, invites you to a
  family event, or offers to pay you directly for extra hours outside the agency. They are
  warm, you get on well, and saying no feels like rejecting somebody who has been kind to
  you.
setting: home
audience: [RBT, BCaBA, BCBA]
riskFlags: []
steps:
  - text: Decline the personal connection kindly and without a long explanation, and give the reason once.
    rationale: >-
      A short, warm, consistent answer — that you keep work contact through work channels
      for every family — is easier to give and easier to hear than a case-by-case judgment.
  - text: Move anything genuinely useful onto the official channel — the agency phone, the agency email, the scheduled contact.
    rationale: >-
      Families usually want to be able to reach somebody, not to be your friend. Meeting
      the real need makes the boundary much easier to hold.
  - text: Send private work offers to your employer, and do not negotiate them yourself.
    rationale: >-
      Working privately with the same learner usually crosses a contractual restriction and
      always creates a second relationship, with a supervisor who does not know it exists.
  - text: Check what your employer's policy actually says about contact outside sessions, and follow that rather than your own judgment.
    rationale: >-
      This is one of the few places where an employer's rule and the code point the same
      way, and quoting the policy takes the personal sting out of the answer.
  - text: Tell your supervisor that it came up, briefly, before it becomes something you have been managing alone.
    rationale: >-
      Nothing about the request is wrong or shameful. It is ordinary, it will happen again,
      and a supervisor who knows can back you up.
whatNotToDo:
  - Do not accept the request and plan to keep it professional.
  - Do not give a personal number "just for emergencies" — emergencies are exactly when an unmonitored channel causes harm.
  - Do not post anything about your work that could identify a learner, regardless of privacy settings.
  - Do not take private paid work with a family you serve through an employer without written agreement from both.
whenToEscalate:
  - Tell your supervisor about any offer of private work, money, or a gift of significant value.
  - >-
    If a personal relationship already exists — a neighbor, a relative, somebody from your
    own community — say so before the assignment starts rather than afterwards.
